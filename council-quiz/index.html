Which Councilor Votes Like You?

How this works

  • Where the questions come from. Each statement describes the outcome of one real roll-call vote where at least one councilor voted no, so a councilor who voted for the proposal "agrees" with the statement and one who voted against "disagrees." Unanimous votes are left out because they can't tell councilors apart.
  • It's a simplification. A councilor can vote for or against a proposal for reasons that have nothing to do with the statement (the details of the bill, who sponsored it, an amendment that failed). Treat a match as a pointer to their record, not a verdict. The results link to each councilor's full voting record on How Your Councilor Voted.
  • Checked against the official minutes. Every vote in the quiz was checked against the named yes and no lists in the Council's minutes. Resolutions the minutes record as voice votes, with no names, are left out.
  • Absences and abstentions aren't counted as disagreement. A councilor needs at least 8 shared answers with you to be ranked. Councilors who joined recently have fewer votes to compare.
  • The 2026 budget was voted on in separate sections so members with conflicts could abstain; those sections follow nearly the same party-line pattern, so they're counted as separate questions but say little about individual councilors beyond the budget fight itself.
  • The notes after your results summarize city records and published news coverage, with a link to each source. They describe what happened; they don't endorse any policy. Where no results were reported, the note says so.
  • Names in older records. The 2021–2023 records list a single "Brown," which is Ali Brown (Jesse Brown joined the Council in 2024). Crista Lee Wells appears in the records as Crista Carlino through 2025; her votes under both names are combined.
