CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 10, 2025
Proposal No. 112, 2025

A SPECIAL RESOLUTION celebrating the legacy of Ernie Pyle.

WHEREAS, Ernest Taylor Pyle was a true Hoosier. Born August 3, 1900, on a farm south of Dana in
Vermiilion County before he grew and became the preeminent correspondent during World Ward tl; and

WHEREAS, prior to that worldwide conflict, Pyle developed a national audience through his Hoosier
Vagabond columns written for Scripps Howard Newspapers as he and his wife traveled across the United
States, Central America and South America during the Great Depression; and

WHEREAS, Pyle’s self-described “worm’s eye” view of the war served as a bridge between the front
line troops and their home front families, who were starved for news about their husband, sons, brothers,
uncles and cousins. He wrote about the common soldiers’ lives, not war strategy or American generals.
His columns were published in 700 newspapers; and

WHEREAS, Pyle will be forever linked to American veteran as the successful advocate of “fight pay”
for servicemen who experienced combat. Congress listened to him and passed in May 1944, the Ernie
Pyle Bill, which established extra pay for those stationed in a combat zone. That amount is now 50 percent
more than normal pay. Combat pay has continued to be U.S. policy in every conflict after WWII; and

WHEREAS, Ernie Pyle’s devotion to the troops put him in harm’s way. He was nearly d by a
German artillery shell in Anzio and by friendly fire from American B-17’s during the breakout from the
hedgerow country at St. Lo; and

WHEREAS, despite his fears he would not return, Pyle determined he had to cover the pacific War
after several years of following the war in Europe. He was with the troops during the invasion of North
Africa, Sicily, Italy, Normandy, and witnessed the liberation of Paris; and

WHEREAS, his life ended on the island of le Shima, from a bullet fired from a Japanese machine gun
on April 18, 1945, just five months before the war ended with the surrender of Japan. Pyle was 44 years
old; and

WHEREAS, upon his death President Harry Truman said, “No man in this war has so well told the story
of the American fighting man as American fighting me wanted it told. He deserves the gratitude of all hi
countrymen,” and

WHEREAS, on this 80" anniversary of both his passing and the subsequent recognition of having VFW
Post 1120, established on the 8" of July 1945, named in his honor, now, therefore.

BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA:

SECTION 1. The Indianapolis City-County Council honors the life of Ernie Pyle, the soldier's friend and
Pulitzer Prize-winning journalist.

SECTION 2. The Council celebrates the memory of Ernie Pyle for his many accomplishments.
SECTION 3. The Mayor is invited to join in this resolution by affixing his signature hereto.

SECTION 4. This resolution shall be in full force and effect upon adoption and compliance with IC 36-3-4-
14,
