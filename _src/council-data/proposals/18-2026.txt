CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO.      , 2026 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing the  Indianapolis Ten Point Coalition for playing a vital role in 
helping to reduce urban youth violence in the Indianapolis community for over 26 years.   
 
WHEREAS, the Indianapolis Ten Point Coalition (ITPC) is a branch of the National Ten Point Coalition 
organized in Boston in 1992 , and is recognized as one of the most successful faith -based anti-violence 
groups in the U.S .  The Indianapolis branch was established in January 1999 by 15 black churches as a 
faith-based 501(c)(3) initiative to help address urban youth violence among black males ages 12 -24; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Coalition is a clergy -led effort to bring together faith -based organizations, community 
leaders, businesses, law enforcement, and the courts in order to create safer, more resilient communities 
by addressing the root causes of violence and providing support and resources to at -risk individuals; and 
 
WHEREAS, ITPC’s initial focus was to reduce youth violence in the 46208 and 46205 zip codes, 
where the Indy Ten Point churches are located. The Coalition began with faith patrols in high-crime areas 
in these neighborhoods on Friday and Saturday nights. Today, working in partnership with churches and 
other community groups, the Indy Coalition has patrol teams on the streets seven days a week, 52 weeks 
a year, regardless of the weather, expanding to the far eastside and encompassing over 21 different 
hotspot areas identified by IMPD and community stakeholders; and  
 
WHEREAS, the Coalition patrol teams are comprised of former ex-offenders, clergy, and individuals 
who reside in these hotspot patrol areas. Each of these individuals volunteer 10 to 12 hours a week , and 
receives a stipend to cover food and gas expenses for their service; and  
 
WHEREAS, in addition to their regular patrols in high -crime areas, they have worked closely with 
IMPD to deploy a rapid response team to troubled neighborhoods in order to help defuse and de -escalate 
violent crime and retaliation .  ITPC believes that grassroots efforts and strong relationships with at -risk 
males of color ages 12 -24 can effectively lower violence in high -risk areas.  "One group is not the silver 
bullet to end youth violence in the city, but when we start working together and bringing our different tools 
to the table, then that's when our city has been most successful,” stated co -founder Rev. Charles 
Harrison; and

criminal homicides among this demographic. Since the ir founding in 1999 , there have been 80 times
