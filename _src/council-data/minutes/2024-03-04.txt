CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 7, 2024 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing March as Women’s History Month.   
 
WHEREAS, March is celebrated as Women’s History Month; a time to celebrate all women, the advances they have 
made, and understand how far they still must go to reach full equality with men; and 
 
WHEREAS, Hoosier Women have always been on the forefront of fighting for their rights, we celebrate those who 
have made it possible to have a record-breaking number of seven female Councilor; and

WHEREAS, Indiana was the first state after Seneca Falls to have an organized suffrage movement. We celebrate 
the women who called to convention, held October 14- 15, 1851 in Dublin, Indiana, where they adopted resolutions for 
political, social, and financial rights for women; and 
 
WHEREAS, the 1852 conventio n, also held in Henry County, Indiana, formed the Indiana Woman's Rights 
Association to promote united action for woman's rights. The Association's 1853 convention demanded equality in all 
political rights and functions. It voted to be an auxiliary to the American Woman Suffrage Association in 1870, and 
became the Indiana Woman's Suffrage Association; and  
 
WHEREAS, we celebrate great suffragists such as Dr. Mary Thomas of Richmond, Indiana, who after hearing a 
female abolitionist and feminist at a Quaker m eeting in 1845, joined the Indiana Woman’s Rights Society, where she 
served as president. In 1859, she was the first woman to speak to Indiana’s state legislature when she presented a petition 
calling for a married women’s property law and women’s right to vote. It was reported that the chambers did not take her 
seriously. Thomas wrote in the petition, “This political oppression is crushing women, and we feel to repeat that the time 
has come when the women of Indiana should calmly and dispassionately assert  their right to the elective franchise, and 
the privileges growing out of it, as the basis of all the other rights;” and 
 
WHEREAS, we celebrate Indianapolis’ May Wright Sewall, a teacher and head of the Indianapolis Girls Classical 
School, formed the Propylaeum as a place where women could connect and share ideas. Sewall served as chairman of 
the National Woman’s Suffrage Association's executive committee from 1882 to 1890 and was the organization's first 
recording secretary. She also served as president of  the National Council of Women of the United States from 1897 to 
1899, and president of the International Council of Women from 1899 to 1904. In addition, she helped organize the 
General Federation of Women's Clubs, and served as its first vice-president. Sewall was also an organizer of the World's 
Congress of Representative Women, which was held in conjunction with the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago 
in 1893. U.S. President William McKinley appointed her as a U.S. representative of women to the Exposition Universelle 
(1900) in Paris. She also founded the Indianapolis Museum of Art, now Newfields, and Herron School of Art; and 
 
WHEREAS, we celebrate Carrie Barnes Ross, a black woman and resident of Indianapolis, and the other members 
of the Equal Suffrage Association Branch 7 of Indiana, who were the first suffrage organization in Indiana to be led and 
focused on black women’s suffrage. On June 25, 1912, in the home of Madam C.J. Walker, at 640 North West Street, 
Barnes became one of the founders of Branch 7 of the Equal Suffrage Association. Between 30 and 40 Black women and 
men met to organize the first all -African American-led suffrage organization in Indianapolis. Prominent attorney and 
social activist Freeman Bailey Ransom called the meeting to or der and presided over the election of the organization’s 
officers. Barnes was elected president and served as chairperson of the branch; and 
 
WHEREAS, 104 years later, the women of this Council stand upon the backs of giants who gave of their time, talent, 
treasure, and sometimes their lives, to achieve women’s suffrage. Women were never given the right to vote, they had to 
fight and take it. We understand the gravity of their gifts and carry the weight of their legacy every day; now, therefore:   
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1.  The Indianapolis City-County Council proudly recognizes and celebrates Women’s History Month. 
 
SECTION 2.  The Council recognizes the work and contr ibutions of the women of the City of Indianapolis to live our 
lives daily, and thanks them for their sacrifices. 
 
SECTION 3.  The Mayor is invited to join in this resolution by affixing his signature hereto. 
 
SECTION 4.  This resolution shall be in full force and effect upon adoption and compliance with IC 36-3-4-14.

CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 8, 2024 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing Irish American Heritage Month. 
 
WHEREAS, Indianapolis is home to a vibrant and thriving Irish American community, whose members have made 
significant contributions to the cultural, social, and economic life of our state; and 
 
WHEREAS, Indianapolis has been home to many notable Irish immigrants, people of Irish ancestry, and Irish 
organizations including: Thomas A. Taggart of County Monaghan, Ireland who was elected Mayor of Indianapolis in 
1895, 1897 and 1899; Sculptor John Hawkins Mahoney whose bronze statues of George Rogers Clark, William Henr y 
Harrison and James Whitcomb adorn the Soldiers’ and Sailors’ Monument; and the Ancient Order of Hibernians whose 
motto of “Friendship, Unity and Christian Charity” has long promoted Irish culture and philanthropy in the Indianapolis 
community; and   
 
WHEREAS, the month of March has been recognized as Irish American Heritage Month since 1991, providing an 
opportunity to celebrate the achievements and contributions of Irish Americans; and  
 
WHEREAS, Indianapolis has a rich history of Irish American immigrat ion, with many Irish Americans settling in 
the city during the 19th and 20th centuries, and continuing to make their mark in various fields such as agriculture, 
education, and politics; and  
 
WHEREAS, the Irish American community in Indianapolis has preser ved and shared its unique cultural heritage 
through various activities and events, including music, dance, sport, language, and food, now, therefore: 
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1.  The Indianapolis City-County Council proclaims March as Irish American Heritage Month in Indianapolis 
and encourages all citizens to honor and recognize the significant contributions of Irish Americans to our city, state and 
nation. 
 
SECTION 2.  T he Council recognizes the importance of preserving and promoting the cultural heritage of the Irish 
American community in Indianapolis and encourages individuals and organizations to participate in activities and events 
that celebrate and highlight the contributions of this important community. 
 
SECTION 3. The Council expresses their appreciation and gratitude to the Irish American community in Indianapolis 
for their many contributions to our city, and encourages all residents of Indianapolis to join in celebrating Irish American 
Heritage. 
 
SECTION 4.  The Mayor is invited to join in this resolution by affixing his signature hereto. 
 
SECTION 5.  This resolution shall be in full force and effect upon adoption and compliance with IC 36-3-4-14.
