MONDAY, MAY 2, 2022 
 
The City -County Council of Indianapolis, Marion County, Indiana and the Indianapolis Police 
Special Service District Council, Indianapolis Fire Special Service District Council and 
Indianapolis Solid Waste Collection Special Service District Council convened in regular 
concurrent sessions, in the Council Chamber of the City-County Building at 7:11 p.m. on Monday, 
May 2, 2022 with Councillor Osili presiding. 
 
Councillor Mowery introduced Pastor Tim Lindsey, Public Servants’ Prayer, who led the opening 
prayer.  Councillor Mowery then invited all present to join him in the Pledge of Allegiance to the 
Flag. 
 
ROLL CALL 
 
The President instructed the Clerk to take the roll call and requested members to register their 
presence on the voting machine.  The roll call was as follows: 
 
25 PRESENT: Adamson, Annee, Bain, Barth, Boots, Brown, Carlino, Dilk, Evans -E, Evans-J, 
Graves, Gray, Hart, Jackson, Jones, Larrison, Lewis, Mascari, McCormick, Mowery, Oliver, 
Osili, Potts, Ray, Robinson 
0 ABSENT:  
 
A quorum of twenty-five members being present, the President called the meeting to order. 
 
INTRODUCTION OF GUESTS AND VISITORS 
 
President Osili introduced Dean Sian Mooney , O’Neill School of Public and Environmental 
Affairs, who in turn, introduced Tom Guevara, Director of the IU Public Policy Institute.  Director 
Guevara presented the 2022 John L. Krauss Award for Public Policy Innovation to the Indianapolis-
Marion County Council.  John Krauss, for whom the award was named stated that he served many 
years ago as Deputy Mayor under Former Mayor William Hudnut.  He said that there are two main 
things public servants are charged with doing:  solving problems and creating oppor tunities.  He 
said that the City -County Council has done that by working in a bi -partisan manner to make a 
difference and effect change.  Representatives congratulated the Council for the achievement.  Vice 
President Adamson thanked representatives for the honor and stated that the Council staff is also 
very critical in helping this body achieve their goals and are included in this honor.

The President called for the reading of Official Communications.  The Clerk read the following:

WHEREAS, Hoosiers across the entire state feel the effects of crime; and 
 
WHEREAS, whether they are a victim of a crime or a family  member of someone who has been heavily affected 
by a crime, the repercussions can cause mental, emotional, relational, and physical damage; and 
 
WHEREAS, becoming a victim of or being on the periphery of a crime can be an intimidating, overwhelming, and 
traumatizing process. Victims often choose not to pursue charges because of the re-traumatization that occurs throughout 
the trial process; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Marion County Prosecutor’s Office has the largest Victim Advocate Unit in the state of Indiana to 
assist victims and their families throughout the criminal justice process and address their concerns and needs; and 
 
WHEREAS, Victim Advocates provide victims enduring the criminal justice process with encouragement, empathy, 
and comfort. They assist victims with necessary counseling and additional quality of life services; and 
 
WHEREAS, victims of crime often do not report their victimization due to numerous barriers including intimidation 
from their abuser, fear of the criminal justice system, and family history with the criminal justice system; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Marion County Prosecutor’s Office not only provides supportive services to victims with an active 
case, but connects with all community members regardless of their case status. The Pros ecutor’s Office wants to lessen 
the intimidation that is often paired with reporting a crime; and 
 
WHEREAS, the courage and strength of victims that have endured or are currently enduring the criminal justice 
process is moving. The healing process is not linear but justice for victims can provide an immense amount of relief and 
closure for those affected by crimes; and 
 
WHEREAS, National Crime Victims’ Rights Week provides an opportunity for our community and service 
providers to recognize and honor those a ffected by crimes. We must ensure victims of past and present crimes have a 
voice and feel confident using it to share their stories; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Marion County Prosecutor’s Office is proud to recognize National Crime Victims’ Rights Week 
2022 and hon ored to serve our victims and survivors when dealing with the trauma of a crime. We will continue our 
dedicated service and commitment to servicing diverse victim populations; now, therefore: 
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1.  The Indianapolis City -County Council is proud to join with the Marion County Prosecutor’s Office to 
recognize April 24-30, 2022 as National Crime Victims’ Rights Week. 
 
SECTION 2.  The Council urges the Consolidated City of Indianapolis to serve and support those citizens in our 
community who have been affected by crime. This includes victims and survivors and their families and friends who are 
providing encouragement, empathy, and resources to help o vercome the trauma; and working with the various public 
safety and court agencies to put processes and programs in place to seek justice for victims and prevent further such 
occurrences. 
 
SECTION 3.  The Mayor is invited to join in this resolution by affixing his signature hereto. 
 
SECTION 4.  This resolution shall be in full force and effect upon adoption and compliance with IC 36-3-4-14.

ARTICLE III. ADOPTION AND INTERPRETATION 
 
Sec. 152-301. Exhibits. 
 
The map or maps filed in connection with the submission of the districting plan adopted as Article II of this Chapter 
shall be marked by the clerk as "Proposal No.157 2022; Exhibit A" and retained with the permanent records of the 
ordinance adopting this article. 
 
Sec. 152-302. Rules of construction. 
 
(a) Any discrepancies or conflicts between the geographic boundaries of the precincts and the boundaries of census 
blocks and any omissions or duplications shall be resolved by reference first to the rules of construction stated in this 
section and if not resolved by such reference, then to the individual district map or maps referenced as exhibits in section 
152-301. 
 
(b) If a conflict exists between a map or maps referenced as an exhibit in Section 152-301 showing the boundaries 
of a district and a description of the boundaries of that district set forth in the descriptions of the plan in Article II of this 
Chapter, the district boundaries are the descriptions of the boundaries set forth in the plan in Article II of this Chapter, 
not the boundaries shown on the map or maps referenced as an exhibit in Section 152-301, but only to the extent there is 
a conflict between the description and the map or maps. 
 
(c) If a conflict exists between the overall map of the districts and any map of a single district referenced as exhibits 
in Section 152-301, the district boundaries shall be those shown in the individual district map to the extent there is no 
conflict between such map and the district plan set forth in Article II of this Chapter. 
 
(d) The districts in Art icle II of this Chapter shall be construed so as to include all of the territory of the county 
within one (1), but only one (1), of the districts established by this article. 
 
(e) Article II of this Chapter shall be construed so as to exclude from all dist ricts any territory not located in the 
county. 
 
(f) If any territory in the county is not included in one (1) of the districts, the territory is included in the district that 
(i) is contiguous to that territory and (ii) contains the least population of all districts contiguous to that territory.  
 
(g) If any territory in the county is included in more than one (1) of the districts, the territory is included in the 
district that (i) is one (1) of the districts in which the territory is described in Article II of this Chapter, (ii) is contiguous 
to that territory and (iii) contains the least population of all the districts contiguous to that territory. 
 
(h) Any deviation of precinct boundaries from census block boundaries shall be resolved by using the census block 
boundary. 
 
Sec. 152-303. Reservations. 
 
The council reserves the right to amend or revise the descriptions of the districts set forth in Article II of this Chapter 
to correct any errors, duplications or omissions in the descriptions, to conform to any corrections or revisions of the 
census data hereafter made by the U.S. Bureau of Census, to comply with any valid court order respecting this article, or 
for any other lawful purpose. 
 
Sec. 152-304.  
 
The districts established in Sec. 152-201 through 152-225 shall be in effect for elections to be held in 2023 and thereafter.
