CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 6, 2022 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing Black History Month. 
 
 WHEREAS, Africans were first brought involuntarily to the shores of the United States as early as the 17th century; 
and 
 
 WHEREAS, by 1827, 6 years after the founding of Indianapolis, 55 of the City’s 1,066 residents were Black, 
intertwining Black history and Indianapolis history since the City’s earliest days; and 
 
 WHEREAS, as a people, Black Americans have s urvived enslavement, lynch mobs, segregation, legalized 
discrimination, and denial of the basic and fundamental rights of citizenship, and nonetheless secured their freedom and 
have risen to serve Indianapolis, the State of Indiana, and the United States at the highest levels of government, business, 
academia, philanthropy, and the military; and  
 
 WHEREAS, the contributions of Black Americans  from all walks of life throughout the history of this City have 
made unforgettable marks on Indianapolis and the world as artists, scientists, educators, entertainers, entrepreneurs, 
athletes, thought leaders, faith leaders, and political and governmental  leaders; and whereas Black residents of 
Indianapolis have been essential to the City’s growth, vibrancy, and success on the world stage; and  
 
WHEREAS, those leaders have included the likes of Madam C.J. Walker , Marshall “Major” Taylor, Freeman 
Ransom, Noble Sissle, Wes Montgomery, Father Boniface Hardin, Sister Edna Mae Barnes Martin, Mari Evans, Oscar 
Robertson, George McGinnis, Angela Brown, Kenneth “Babyface” Edmonds, Cleo Blackburn, Amos Brown, Julia 
Carson, and too many more to name; and 
 
WHEREAS, Indianapolis has been the backdrop for important developments in Black history, including being home 
to one of the most active “stops” on the Underground Railroad at the historic Bethel AME Church; the 1895 founding of 
the Indianapolis Recorder, the fourth-oldest surviving Black newspaper in the United States; the rise of Indiana Avenue 
as a national destination for jazz musicians and singers; hosting, and winning, the first game of the inaugural season of 
the Negro National Baseball League, played on May 2, 1920 at Washington Park in Indianapolis; and 
 
WHEREAS, in spite of these notable individuals, accomplishments, and places, many Black Americans in 
Indianapolis and elsewhere have lived, toiled, and died in obscurity, never achieving the recognition they de served but 
still paving the way for future generations to succeed; and 
 
 WHEREAS, in the face of injustices, people of good will and of all races in the Indianapolis and the United States 
have distinguished themselves with a commitment to the noble ideals on which the United States was founded and have 
fought courageously for the rights and freedom of Black Americans and others; now, therefore: 
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1. This Council acknowledges that all people of Indianapolis and of the United States are the recipients of the 
wealth of history provided by Black culture. 
 
SECTION 2. The Council likewise recognizes the significa nce of Black History Month as an important op portunity to 
commemorate the tremendous contributions of Black Americans to the history of our City, State, and nation. 
 
SECTION 3. The Council encourages the celebration of Black History Month to provide a continuing opportunity for all 
people in our community to learn from the past and understand the experiences of the Black Americans who have shaped 
Indianapolis.  
 
SECTION 3. The Mayor is invited to join in this resolution by affixing his signature hereto. 
 
SECTION 4. This resolution shall be in full force and effect upon adoption and compliance with IC 36-3-4-14.

Proposal No. 78, 2022 was retitled SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 7, 2022, and reads as follows: 
 
CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 7, 2022 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing Teen Dating Violence Awareness Month.  
 
 WHEREAS, dating violence is defined as a pattern of assaultive and controlling behaviors used by one person 
against another in order to gain or maintain power and control in a relationship; and 
 
 WHEREAS, dating violence is very common, especially among teens and young adults: one in three teens in the 
US will experience physical, sexual, or emotional abuse from someone they are in a relationship with; and   
  
 WHEREAS, Indiana faces higher than average rates of teen dating violence. One in ten Hoosier teens face physical 
violence in their relationship and one in eight have experienced sexual violence in their relationship; and  
  
 WHEREAS, teen dating violence can display itself in multiple forms. Teens facing violence in their relationships 
may be subject to physical, sexual, or psychological violence, including stalking; and   
  
 WHEREAS, violence and aggressive behaviors in adole scent intimate relationships can create unhealthy 
relationship patterns which often continue throughout life. Supporting the development of healthy, respectful, and 
nonviolent relationships can help reduce the occurrence of teen dating violence and prevent its harmful effects; and   
  
 WHEREAS, during the pre-teen and teen years, it is important for youth to begin learning the skills needed to create 
and maintain healthy relationships; and 
 
 WHEREAS, teen dating violence impacts youth and families of all backgrounds across Indianapolis; and  
  
 WHEREAS, placing value and importance on local organizations that work to educate and assist youth about the 
signs and effects of unsafe relationships is crucial to ensuring the safety of our teens; and  
  
 WHEREAS, the Marion County Prosecutor’s Office recognizes this social problem and has secured a multi -year 
public grant to provide a dedicated deputy prosecutor to educate youth on the issue and prosecute related cases; and  
  
 WHEREAS, February is National Teen Dat ing Violence Awareness and Prevention Month, and because the 
residents of Indianapolis of all ages are deserving of healthy family and partner relationships; now, therefore: 
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1. The City-County Council recognizes the vital role it plays in supporting the wellbeing of our City’s youth 
population with appropriate activities and programs aimed at preventing and recognizing all forms of dating abuse. 
 
SECTION 2. The Council similarly recognizes the toll that dating violence takes on youth and families in Indianapolis, 
and embraces its civil duty to help Indianapolis teens gain the skills and knowledge needed to form healthy and safe 
relationships. 
 
SECTION 3. The Mayor is invited to join in this resolution by affixing his signature hereto. 
 
SECTION 4. This resolution shall be in full force and effect upon adoption and compliance with IC 36-3-4-14.
