CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 19, 2021 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing the 125th anniversary of Lauck & Veldhof Funeral & Cremation Services. 
 
WHEREAS, Lauck & Veldhof Funeral & Cremation Services has been serving the southside, downtown and greater 
Indianapolis community since its inception in 1896 by Anthony J. Lauck; and 
 
WHEREAS, the first location was established on S. Meridian Street where I-70 currently crosses and was moved to 
its present location in the early 1920s by Anthony P. Lauck, A.J.’s son; and  
 
WHEREAS, Lauck & Veldhof Funeral and Cremation Services’ current location was originally part of a large farm 
stretching back to West Street and the railroad tracks. The original part of the house was built in 1877 and had additions 
added in 1954 and 1960; and 
  
WHEREAS, new additions were added by Leo Lauck, Sr., who was quite active in the funeral business and 
community until his passing in 1972. The chapel he added in 1 960 was one of the largest at that time and will seat 130 
people. His son, Leo, Jr., then took over the family business in the early 1970s and continues to help, even though the 
business was sold to Jeff Veldhof in 2010. Jeff Veldhof completely remodeled t he facility inside and out and brought 
everything up to date; and 
 
WHEREAS, Lauck & Veldhof believes in being an active part of our community, as Jeff Veldof is a member of 
both St. Roch and Sacred Heart Churches; and he serves on the parish council at St.  Roch and on the board of the Old 
Southside Neighborhood Association, as a member of the Stadium Village Business Association, and as a block captain 
with Keep Indianapolis Beautiful; and 
  
WHEREAS, providing comfort for neighborhood families in their time  of need is the main concern of the staff at 
Lauck & Veldhof. They are a small family -owned firm and take great pride in the personal services they offer; now, 
therefore: 
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1.  The Indianapolis City -County Council proudly recognizes Lauck and Veldhof Funeral and Cremation 
Services on their 125th Anniversary.  
 
SECTION 2.  The Council extends best wishes for continued success to Lauck & Veldhof Funeral & Cremation Services. 
 
SECTION 3.  The Mayor is invited to join in this resolution by affixing his signature hereto. 
 
SECTION 4.  This resolution shall be in full force and effect upon adoption and compliance with IC 36-3-4-14.

CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 21, 2021 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION condemning all forms of racist harassment, violence, or bias against the Asian American 
and Pacific Islander community. 
 
WHEREAS, all communities of color, including Black and Brown people, indigenous people, Asian Americans and 
Pacific Islanders, and generally all racial, ethnic, socio-economic and social minorities, are at risk of discrimination; and 
 
WHEREAS, all forms of racism and ethnocentrism constitute a major threa t to equity, diversity, inclusivity and 
social justice in the lives of black and brown people, indigenous people, Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders , and 
generally all racial, ethnic, socio -economic and social minorities , in terms of education, justice,  environment, 
employment and economy, income, housing, health and health care, and safety; and  
 
WHEREAS, on February 20th of 2020, the members of this Council unanimously adopted a proposal declaring 
racism to be unjust, reiterating our support for equal rights for all and affirming the City's commitment to ending 
disparities based on race, place, or identity; and 
 
WHEREAS, racism and ethnocentrism have a negative impact on the wellbeing of individuals, families, and the 
entire Indianapolis community; and 
 
WHEREAS, 29,429 Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders who represent 3.4% of Marion County’s total population 
today call Indianapolis home; and  
 
WHEREAS, March 11, 2021 marked the one-year anniversary of the declaration of COVID-19 as a global pandemic 
and the beginning of a surge in anti-Asian harassment and violence nationwide; and  
 
WHEREAS, over 2 million Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders are working on the frontlines of this COVID–19 
pandemic in health care, law enforcement, first responders, tran sportation, supermarkets, and other service industries; 
and 
 
WHEREAS, a PerryUndem November 2020 survey, found that 1 in 6 Asian American and Pacific Islander voters 
say they or a family member have faced racism related to COVID -19, with the targets of the se incidents often being 
elders, women, or low-income people; and  
 
WHEREAS, the Stop AAPI Hate Reporting Center received information on 3,795 incidents of racism directed at 
Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders in the United States between March 19, 2020  to February 28, 2021, including 
reports of verbal harassment, physical assault, civil rights violations, and online harassment occurring in all 50 States and 
the District of Columbia; and  
 
WHEREAS, the Indiana Chapter of the National Asian Pacific American Women’s Forum reports that “One of the 
most devastating effects of the pandemic has been the dramatic spike in discrimination, bigotry, and violence towards 
AAPI Hoosiers” and that across Indiana, children have been bullied at school, and families going outdoors and employees 
going to work have faced hostility, verbal abuse, and violence”; and 
 
WHEREAS, 46 Indiana organizations and institutions based in the state have joined a call for action from the Indiana 
Chapter of the National Asian Pacific American Women’s Forum, including Indiana University, the City of Bloomington, 
Butler University, Indiana American Chinese Association, Indiana American Chinese Association, Korean American 
Association of Indiana, Faith in Indiana,  Greater Indianapolis Multifaith Alliance, Indiana Muslim Advocacy Network, 
Indianapolis Jewish Community Relations Council, Marion County Commission on Youth, Immigration Justice Task 
Force, Women4Change, and Indiana National Organization for Women; and

SPECIAL ORDERS - PUBLIC HEARING 
 
PROPOSAL NO. 116, 2021.  Councillor Robinson report ed that the Public Safety and Criminal 
Justice Committee heard Proposal No. 116, 2021 on March 24, 2021.  The proposal, sponsored by 
Councillor Robinson, approves transfers and additional appropriations totalling $2,693,500 in the 
2021 Budget of the Office of Public Health and Safety (Consolidated County General and Federal 
Grants Funds) for the purposes of funding operations of the James E. Waters Jr. Community 
Mediation Center, non -congregate housing, and personal protective gear and additional supplies 
for the Community Violence Reduction Team .  By a 10-0 vote, the Committee reported the 
proposal to the Council with the recommendation that it do pass.   
 
Councillor Hart asked if this expense will be reimbursed by federal dollars.  Matt Giffin, interim 
director of the Office of Public Health and Safety (OPHS), said that $2.4 million is 100% 
reimbursable by FEMA, and moving it to this fund allows them to extend this program for three 
months without additional cost to the city.   
 
Councillor Gray asked where it this is located and what kind of mediation is performed.  Julie 
Fidler, director, said that this is community mediation, not mediation by a lawyer, and they try to 
help neighbors having conflicts from becoming a situation that escalates to violence.  This is guided 
by volunteers and an advisory board made up of community members.  It is located at E. 10th Street 
and Rural Street, across from the Marathon Gas Station.   
 
Councillor Annee said that this is a great way to acknowledge James Waters and is a great tribute 
to him and all his efforts to reduce violent crime in the city. 
 
Councillors Adamson and Jackson echoed Councillor Annee’s comments and said that he was an 
enormous loss to this city and community.   
 
The President called for public testimony at 8:25 p.m.  There being no one present to testify,
