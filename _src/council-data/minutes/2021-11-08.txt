President Osili asked in honor of Veteran’s Day this week that all in attendance and those watching 
at home pause for a mo ment of silent gratitude for our nation's veterans, remembering that the 
democratic process in which we participate tonight was, and is, secured through the bravery and 
sacrifice of all those who have served. 
 
Councillor Hart introduced Pastor Denell Howard, Hovey Street Church, who led the opening 
prayer.  Councillor Hart then invited all present to join him in the Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag.

CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 33, 2021 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION honoring Norman Pace for his dedication and commitment to the residents of Warren 
Township and the City of Indianapolis. 
 
WHEREAS, Norman Pace was born in Indianapolis, IN, and has lived in Warren Township for over 50 years; and 
 
WHEREAS, in 1990, Norman witnessed a group of concerned citizens lose their attempt to stop a rezoning. 
Realizing this loss was not due to lack of cause, but rather a lack of procedural knowledge, Norman stepped forward and 
went to work. He attended meetings , watched proceedings, and learned to navigate the often-confusing waters of 
governmental protocol; and 
 
WHEREAS, using his knowledge, Norman became founder and President of the Far Eastside Neighborhood 
Association, FENA, which eventually reached membership of over 300. He earned the reputation of a “go-to” guy whose 
expertise and insight was routinely sought. Norman met with other neighborhood leaders, offering instruction and 
assistance in starting their own associations or with procedural questions; and 
 
WHEREAS, Norman also served as President of Warren Township Development Association (WTDA), and as 
Land Use Chair with Marion County Alliance of Neighborhood Associations (MCANA), and volunteered to serve on 
numerous committees. Wearing his signature “Warren Pride” white hat, he traversed the city to meet with business 
owners, attorneys, neighborhood associations, developers, and concerned citizens to provide assistance in safeguarding 
the community; and

CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 36, 2021 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION to endorse and urge the United States Congress to pass the Healthcare Emergency Guarantee 
Act of 2020 ( HR 6906, SB 2790) and the M edicare for A ll Act of 2019 ( HR 1384, SB 1129), to provide universal, 
comprehensive health coverage with zero cost-sharing during the Covid-19 pandemic and beyond. 
 
WHEREAS, it is vital to the well-being and productivity of residents of Indianapolis to have quality healthcare; and  
 
WHEREAS, before the pandemic, it is estimated that more than half a million H oosiers did not have any health 
insurance; and 
 
WHEREAS, many residents of Indianapolis who do have health insurance are underinsured , resulting in medical 
expenses that exceed their ability to pay; and 
 
WHEREAS, in Indianapolis, about 31.8% of children under 6 and about 20.1% of residents overall live below the 
federal poverty level, and people living in poverty often forgo needed healthcare because of inability to pay; and 
 
WHEREAS, over half the family bankruptcies in Indiana are caused by inability to pay well -being productivity of 
restaurants for medical expenses; and 
 
WHEREAS, most health insurance plans do not cover denta l care and many plans do not have adequate coverage 
of preventive sexual and reproductive health, mental health and substance abuse treatment; and

WHEREAS, the Covid pandemic has underscored the importance of every person having healthcare, because many 
who get sick cannot afford care, continuing to work while contagious ; thus, at the same time, endangering their health 
and spreading the disease to others; and 
 
WHEREAS, there are great health disparities in I ndiana, as in the rest of the U nited States, demonstrated by high 
rates of maternal and infant mortality among black residents, as well as their higher rates of illness and death from Covid-
19; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Affordable Care Hct and th e Healthy Indiana Plan have improved access to healthcare,  but still 
leave millions of people, including thousands in our community, without adequate healthcare coverage; and 
 
WHEREAS, in 2022, the City of Indianapolis is projected to spend $91.8 million on health insurance and related 
costs for its employees, which is approximately 6.8% of the total city budget; and it is expected that these costs would be 
substantially reduced in plans to fund Medicare for All; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Healthcare Emergency Guarantee Act of 2020, proposed in the u.s. congress, would ensure access 
to comprehensive healthcare services during the covid-19 pandemic regardless of ability to pay; and 
 
WHEREAS, the medicare for all act of 2019, proposed in the u.s. congress, would establish a universal single-payer 
health care system that would provide healthcare for all residents of the united states, without fees, copays or deductibles, 
regardless of pre-existing conditions, and with free choice of healthcare providers; and  
 
WHEREAS, the medicare for all act of 2019 provides a comprehensive h ealthcare plan that includes coverage of 
dental care, hearing and vision care, mental healthcare, substance abuse treatment, reproductive and maternity services, 
and prescription drugs; and 
 
WHEREAS, the medicare for all act of 2019 would reduce overall healthcare costs by negotiating drug prices with 
manufactures and eliminating payments to insurance companies; now, therefore: 
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1.  The Indianapolis City-County Council supports the establishment of a comprehensive, universal, single -
payer health care system, with zero cost-sharing, in the United States. 
 
SECTION 2.  The Council w ill advocate for the passage of the E mergency Healthcare Guarantee Act of 2020 a nd the 
Medicare for All Act of 2019. 
 
SECTION 3.  The Council w ill convey its support of these bills to the residents of Indianapolis and to the Indiana 
Congressional Delegation. 
 
SECTION 4.  This resolution shall be in full force and effect upon adoption and compliance with IC 36-3-4-14.

CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL ORDINANCE NO. 20, 2021 
 
PROPOSAL FOR A SPECIAL ORDINANCE approving an interlocal cooperation agreement between the Consolidated City 
of Indianapolis and Marion County, Indiana (“Indianapolis”) and the City of Beech Grove, Indiana (“Beech Grove”) to grant 
Beech Grove a license to use and occupy a portion of the public right -of-way of Thompson Road for the construction, 
operation, continuation and maintenance of a multi-use path. 
 
WHEREAS, Indianapolis and Beech Grove are each a municipality, and pursuant to IC 36-1-3-9(a), each has exclusive 
jurisdiction over the streets inside their respective boundaries; and 
 
WHEREAS, Beech Grove has planned and designed a public improvement project to construct a multi-use path known 
as Thompson Trail, a segment of which is planned to run parallel with and adjacent to Thompson Road on the north side of 
and within the right-of-way of Thompson Road from South 9th Street in Beech Grove to a connection with an existing sidewalk 
near Emerson Avenue in Indianapolis; and 
 
WHEREAS, Thompson Road between Emerson Avenue and Gray Road runs along the boundary between Indianapolis 
and Beech Grove, is designated as a thoroughfare by the Marion County Thoroughfare Plan, is a street under the jurisdiction 
of Indianapolis, is heavily traveled and serves the transportation systems and economic well-being of both Indianapolis and 
Beech Grove; and 
 
WHEREAS, pursuant to IC 36-1-3-9, a municipality may only exercise its powers outside of its boundaries and inside 
the boundaries of another municipality pursuant to an interlocal cooperation agreement between the two municipalities under 
I.C. 36-1-7; and 
 
WHEREAS, accordingly, Beech Grove requires the permission of Indianapolis to use the Thompson Road right-of-way 
for the trail pursuant to an interlocal cooperation agreement and has made a request to do so; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Indianapolis Department of Public Works has considered Beech Grove’s request and has determined 
that it will be of public utility and benefit to grant Beech Grove a license to use the public right-of-way of Thompson Road to 
construct, operate, continue and maintain Thompson Trail; and 
 
WHEREAS, Indianapolis and Beech Grove desire to enter into an interlocal cooperation agreement granting Beech 
Grove a license to use the Thompson Road right-of-way for the trail; and 
 
WHEREAS, IC 36-1-7-4 requires that interlocal cooperation agreements of this nature require the approval of the fiscal 
body of each municipality by ordinance; and 
 
WHEREAS, the City-County Council is the fiscal body of Indianapolis pursuant to IC 36-1-2-6; and 
 
WHEREAS, the City-County Council, having considered the matter and being duly advised, finds that it will be of 
public utility and benefit to grant Beech Grove a license to use the Thompson Road right -of-way for the trail; now, 
therefore: 
 
BE IT ORDAINED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1.  The City-County Council hereby approves the Interlocal Cooperation Agreement in substantially the form 
attached hereto as Exhibit A, incorporated herein by reference.  The Director of the Department of Public Works is hereby 
authorized to finalize, execute and deliver the Interlocal Cooperation Agreement on behalf of the Consolidated City of 
Indianapolis and Marion County, and the Clerk of the City-County Council is hereby authorized to attest to the Council’s 
approval of the Interlocal Cooperation Agreement.  
 
SECTION 2.  A copy of this Special Ordinance and the Interlocal Cooperation Agreement attached hereto and incorporated 
herein by reference shall be kept on file with the Clerk of the City-County Council for public inspection. 
 
SECTION 3. This Special Ordinance shall be in full force and effect upon its passage by the City -County Council and 
compliance with IC 36-3-4-14.
