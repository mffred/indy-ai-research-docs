CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 1, 2023 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing the work of community partners and City agencies for their efforts toward 
achieving significant violence reduction in 2022. 
 
WHEREAS, in 2021 the Council appropriated the first tranche of American Rescue Plan funding to kick start the 
Mayor’s 3-year violence reduction strategy which began in 2022; and 
 
WHEREAS, this historic investment delivered a total of $150 million for the implementation of a data -driven Gun 
Violence Reduction Strategy, the creation of the 50 person Peacemaker program, and the $45 million Elevation Grant 
program for grassroots organizations managed and distributed by the Indianapolis Foundation; and 
 
WHEREAS, through the Gun Violence Reduction Strategy, the City has been able to identify the  individuals and 
groups at the highest risk of being involved in gun violence and have been able to deploy effective interventions with 
these individuals through the Indy Peace Fellowship; and 
 
WHEREAS, from the Violence Interrupters responding to shooting scenes to mediate and develop relationships 
with individuals in conflict, to the Outreach Workers who identify the very high risk individuals to connect them with 
the Life Coaches who help the fellows develop and implement Life Plans, the City’s Peacemakers have utilized their 
experience, knowledge and dedication to our communities to change the lives of our citizens; and  
 
WHEREAS, in 2022, this team of committed individuals interrupted 654 conflicts, provided support at 310 shooting 
scenes, enrolled 99 individuals in the fellowship, attended over 2,000 community events, and referred 1,254 non-fellow 
community members to services; and  
 
WHEREAS, additional grassroots supports have come by the way of the Elevation Grant program, with $14 million 
awarded in 2022 to over 70 organizations, with future award rounds in 2023 and 2024; and  
  
WHEREAS, the City’s efforts extend to other 2022 Operational Budget items like the continued investments in the 
$300,000 Community-Based Violence Prevention Partnership with t his year’s awards going toward New B.O.Y., 
Brightwood Community Center, and VOICES; and   
 
WHEREAS, as a part of the ARPA distribution, $9 million has been dedicated to the investment in modern policing 
technology for the Indianapolis Metropolitan Police D epartment which has seen the successful deployment of 6 new 
public safety cameras that can be moved throughout the County and over 200 license plate readers; and 
 
WHEREAS, through the steadfast efforts by the Peacemakers, law enforcement, and community par tners, our 
community is starting to see the benefits of these investments; and 
 
WHEREAS, just after its first year of implementation of the 3-year strategy, the Mayor announced on January 1 that 
the City has seen the largest single-year reduction in criminal homicides since the creation of IMPD, the lowest level of 
deadly violence since 2019; and 
 
WHEREAS, it’s important to recognize the work dedicated by so many and this achievement however our efforts 
must not stop now—our communities deserve to see continued years of significant reduction of violence and to be without 
the risk of suffering from violence; now, therefore 
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1.  The City -County Council thanks the community partners, law enforcement, and City agencies for their 
work and dedication to making our City a better place for all. 
 
SECTION 2. The City-County Council remembers and grieves the losses of the individuals in our community who have 
been lost due to senseless acts of violence. 
 
SECTION 3. The City-County Council commits to continue to make investments that benefit our communities and make 
them a safer, healthier place. 
 
SECTION 4.  The Mayor is invited to join in this resolution by affixing his signature hereto.

CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 4, 2023 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing Nicole Martinez-LeGrand. 
 
WHEREAS, Nicole Martinez-LeGrand is nativ e Hoosier and Mexican -American who is a direct descendant of 
Indiana's pioneering Mexican colony that began in Northwest Indiana the late 1910s. She has served the City of 
Indianapolis as a community builder for the Mid North Quality of Life Plan convened by The Children's Museum of 
Indianapolis in the early 2010s and recently as a member of Board of Public Works in 2022; and 
 
WHEREAS, in 2016, the Indiana Historical Society sought to fill in a crucial historical gap in its 186 -year-old 
collection, the missing narratives and voices of Indiana's largest growing demographics, Latino and Asians. Since that 
time Nicole had been leading their multicultural collecting initiatives as the Indiana Historical Society's Multicultural 
Collections Curator, through statewide outreach and collecting. The collections comprise of audio recorded interviews, 
genealogical research and hand selected family photographs and documents. All previously unseen in an institution 
setting; and  
 
WHEREAS, since 2018, there were two exhibits, accompanying online exhibits, and traveling exhibits for both 
Latino and Asian communities carefully detailing their history in Indiana. Nicole worked in coordination with Daniel 
Gonzales, Indiana Historical Society's Director of Exhibition Curation; and 
 
WHEREAS, in 2022 the debut publication, Hoosier Latinos: A Century of Struggle Service and Success, outlines 
the personal experiences of Latinos across Indiana through transcribed interviews and personal photos donated to the 
Indiana Historical Society. This book and research uncover some of Indiana's pioneering individuals, families, businesses 
and organizations. It is the first book to address Midwestern Latino identity across the state and not a select region. By 
covering over 100+ years of his tory in Indiana, it combats the common deficit narrative of Latino's as recent arrivals. 
They have been a long part of Indiana's democratic society enriching the quality of life of all Hoosiers.  
 
WHEREAS, Nicole serves as a steward of Indianapolis Latino history through her contributions to the digital 
Encyclopedia of Indianapolis, highlighting long forgotten history such as the early founding of the Mexican Consulate 
in Indianapolis dating back to 1907, Indianapolis's Lost Barrio and Farm worker advocacy during the early years of 
Chicano Civil Rights era.  Nicole is the Multicultural Collections Curator at the Indiana Historical Society where she 
regularly contributes her research to the Indiana Historical Society Blog; now, therefore: 
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1.  The Indianapolis City-County Council proudly recognizes Nicole Martinez-LeGrand. 
 
SECTION 2.  The Council heartily thanks Nicole Martinez-LeGrand for the many hours of research and contributions to 
the Indiana Historic Society her dedicated service to the City of Indianapolis.

WHEREAS, human trafficking is defined as the use of force, threat of force, or coercion to force an individual to 
engage in commercial sex, marriage, labor, criminal activity, or other services. Human trafficking is a pervasive problem 
that affects individuals of all ages from childhood to adulthood; and 
 
WHEREAS, human trafficking is an abhorrent abuse of power and a heinous crime that affects the safety, health, and 
dignity of millions of people locally and globally. Human trafficking is the fastest -growing and second-largest criminal 
industry in the world, generating roughly $150.2 billion worldwide; and 
 
WHEREAS, around the world, there are an estimated 40.3 million victims trapped in modern-day slavery, including 
24.9 million in forced labor and 15.4 million in forced marriage; and 
 
WHEREAS, recent data shows that 2019 saw a 19 percent increase in a single year in human trafficking reports in 
Indiana. In that year alone, 157 human trafficking cases from Indiana were reported to the National Human Trafficking 
Hotline, 40 of which involved minors; and 
 
WHEREAS, Indiana is at high risk for human trafficking because of its specific location as a state with a number of 
intersecting national highways; and 
 
WHEREAS, in spite of these facts, only 10 arrests for human trafficking were made in Indiana in all of 2019; and 
whereas nearly half (46.4 percent) of human trafficking charges in the U.S. were cleared or dropped in 2019; and 
 
WHEREAS, victims' rights groups say that human trafficking across the country has risen during the COVID -19 
pandemic; and 
 
WHEREAS, the 2020 Indiana State Report on Human Trafficking reveals that there have been documented cases 
of forced marriage, forced labor, and sex trafficking of minors and adults across all 92 counties in Indiana; and 
 
WHEREAS, although trafficking has no boundaries for its victims, it disproportionately impacts ethnic and racial 
minorities, women and girls, LGBTQI+ ind ividuals, vulnerable migrants, and other historically marginalized and 
underserved communities, meaning it is essential to continue to conduct preventative outreach to those populations 
identified as at high risk of trafficking; and 
 
WHEREAS, January is National Human Trafficking Prevention Month, and this Council has a civic duty to 
recognize the vital role we play in combating human trafficking and to observe every month with appropriate activities 
and programs aimed at preventing every and all forms of human trafficking; now, therefore: 
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE  
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1. The City-County Council recognizes that human trafficking is a pervasive problem affecting thousands of 
people, including in our own City and State. 
 
SECTION 2. The Council expresses its support for multi -disciplinary partnerships and policy advocacy to alleviate the 
inequities and structural violence that contributes to human trafficking victims feeling trapped in their exploitation. 
 
SECTION 3.  The Mayor is invited to join in this resolution by affixing his signature hereto. 
 
SECTION 4.  This resolution shall be in full force and effect upon adoption and compliance with IC 36-3-4-14.
