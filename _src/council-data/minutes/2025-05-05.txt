Proposal No. 157, 2025 was retitled SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 11, 2025, and reads as follows: 
 
CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 11, 2025 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing the Indy Air Bears jump rope team. 
 
WHEREAS, the Indy Air Bears jump rope team was founded in 1986 by Niki Glover.  Glover began the team with 
14 Arlington Elementary students in grades 2 -5 as an after -school club for fitness. It was first known as the Arlington 
Air Bears, but when jumpers remained on the team through middle and high school, the name was changed to the Indy 
Air Bears; and  
 
WHEREAS, within a year of its inception, the team tripled in size and was being invited to perform at venues all 
around the Indianapolis area.  The Air Bears continue to be based at Arlington Elementary and are annually comprised 
of 35-40 boys and girls from grade 3 to college and beyond. The team has served as ambassadors for the American Heart 
Association in the ‘Jump Rope for Heart’ program since 1987; and 
 
WHEREAS, although originally meant to be a performance team, the Air Bears have found great success over the 
years both in National and International competitions.  The Air Bears have hosted their own Indy Open tournament 
annually since 2000, and have performed in over 230 schools nationwide; and  
 
WHEREAS, in addition, they have performed numerous halftime shows at all levels, including annual shows at Ball 
State, Purdue, Indiana, Butler, and IUPUI, as well as for Indiana Pacers, Indiana Fever, and Indianapolis Colts. They also 
entertained for two Women’s Final Four tournaments, in Indianapolis and in Nashville, TN.  The team has performed in 
the 500 Festival Parade, and several jumpers have performed in four different Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parades, as well 
as in the Rose Bowl Parade; and  
 
WHEREAS, Sonora O’Connor of Shelbyville High School, Bria Mittendorf of Franklin Central High School, 
Brendan Fisher, Ella Fisher  and Owen Willing of Franklin Central High School, Ella Beck of Franklin College, Blake 
Long, Franklin Central High School graduate and Elise McQueen Franklin Central High School/Ball State graduate, are 
members of the current Indy Air Bears jump rope team, who qualified to compete on Team USA in the International 
Jump Rope Union World Championship, which will be held in Kawasaki , Kanagawa, Japan, in July of 2025; now, 
therefore: 
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1.  The Indianapolis City -County Council proudly recognizes the Indy Air Bears Jump Rope team members 
for qualifying to compete on Team USA. 
 
SECTION 2.  The Council wishes the best of luck to the Indy Air Bears Jump Rope team, as they compete at the 
International Jump Rope Union World Championship, which will be held in Kawasaki, Kanagawa, Japan, in July of 
2025. 
 
SECTION 3.  The Mayor is invited to join in this resolution by affixing his signature hereto. 
 
SECTION 4.  This resolution shall be in full force and effect upon adoption and compliance with IC 36-3-4-14.

SECTION 1.  Chapter 135, Article VIII,  of the "Revised Code of the Consolidated City and County," regarding petty cash 
funds, hereby is amended by the addition of a NEW Division 8 to read as follows: 
 
DIVISION 8.  MARION COUNTY TREASURER’S OFFICE PETTY CASH FUNDS 
 
Sec. 135-881.  Created. 
 
Pursuant to IC 36 -1-8-3, the city -county council, as the fiscal body for the Consolidated City of 
Indianapolis and Marion County, Indiana, hereby establishes a petty cash fund in the amount of five hundred 
dollars ($500.00) to be placed in the custody of the Marion County Treasurer (county treasurer) or their 
designee.  The petty cash fund shall be used by the county treasurer’s office to pay small or emergency items 
of operating expenses, such as postage, recording and filing fees, and related administrative uses. 
 
Sec. 135-882.  Administration. 
 
The five-hundred dollars ($500.00) for the treasurer’s petty cash fund established under section 135 -881 
shall be paid by a warrant drawn on the appropriate fund in favor of the Treasurer without the need for an 
appropriation. The Treasurer shall account for the fund in the same manner as other funds and shall return the 
entire fund to the appropriate fund whenever there is a change in the custodian or when the fund is no longer 
needed.

CITY-COUNTY GENERAL RESOLUTION NO. 9, 2025 
 
A GENERAL RESOLUTION designating a portion of 25 th Street from North Hillside Avenue to North Dr. Andrew J. 
Brown Avenue, as Pastor Frank Alexander Memorial Way. 
 
WHEREAS, Pastor Frank Alexander was born June 21, 1943, to Elijah and Annie Alexander and was educated at 
Bishop College in Dallas, Texas and at The Southern Baptist Theological Seminary in Louisville, Kentucky; and 
 
WHEREAS, Pastor Alexander served as an instructor/counselor for Indiana’s Job Corps, as the director of Gateway 
Christian Center in Muncie, Indiana, as the director of Edna Martin Christian Center, and for 40 years he was the senior 
Pastor of Oasis of Hope Baptist Church, a place to serve and be served; and 
 
WHEREAS, under Pastor Alexander’s leadership College Avenue Baptist Church was one of the first Black churches 
in Indianapolis to out-grow its then-current facility and build a new facility named Oasis of Hope Baptist Church; and 
 
WHEREAS, Pastor Alexander led his church in creating two community development corporations, purchasing a 
185-unit apartment complex, building a 70 -unit apartment complex for seniors and providing social services including 
youth employment training, after-school care, and summer day camps; and 
 
WHEREAS, many community and state organizations have benefited from Pastor Alexander’s expertise and 
faithfulness including Martindale/Brightwood Weed and Seed, Martindale/Brightwood Community Resurrection 
Partnership, Jobs Partnership of Greater Indianapo lis, Indiana Healthy Marriage and Family Coalition, and Teacher 
Training for the Indiana Baptist State Convention; and 
 
WHEREAS, Alexander’s reach extended outside of Indianapolis with Oasis Mission for Orphans, Disabled and 
Unaccompanied Children (MODUC), a nonprofit started by Alexander in 1999 to support orphaned children after the 
Liberian Civil War in which he partnere d with Thomas and Mary Beh, who turned their home into an orphanage after 
the conflict.  This mission gave children access to education and medical care; and 
 
WHEREAS, because of the profound affect he had on so many in this community, the city of Indianapolis proclaimed 
November 28, 2015, as Pastor Frank Alexander Day; now, therefore:
