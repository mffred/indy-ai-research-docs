CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 18, 2025 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing the 50th Annual Southside Reunion Picnic. 
 
WHEREAS, the Annual Southside Reunion Picnic, held on August 2, 2025, has been celebrated each year in Babe 
Denny Park for the past 50 years, this resolution honors and commemorates that history. Affectionately known as the 
“First Saturday in August,” this cherished tradition unites generations of the Babe Denny neighborhood, preserving bonds 
of friendship and family that endured even after Interstate 70 displaced this close -knit, historically African American, 
and formerly also Jewish community in the 1970s; and 
 
WHEREAS, the reunion began as William Lester Craig Day in honor of the owner of Craig Funeral Home, whose 
leadership with the men’s Southside Fellowship Club helped sustain family ties. The event was first held at Meikel Street

Park, later renamed Babe Denny Park in 1977 to honor Edward Bay “Babe” Denny, a Southsider, a dedicated city 
employee, and the first Black motorcycle police officer in Indianapolis; and 
 
WHEREAS, through the deep bonds shared by close-knit families and friends, this community has remained strong 
thanks to this annual reunion. Echoes of the past still resonate today as documented in the 2012 publication The 
Neighborhood of Saturdays, which chronicles the oral history of African American and Sephardic Jewish residents who 
lived side by side from the 1910s to the 1960s in this historic and diverse Near Southside neighborhood; and 
 
WHEREAS, the City-County Council of Indianapolis, Indiana, recognizes this 50th Annual Southside Reunion 
Picnic—which included music, laughter, tears of joy, food, activities, guest speakers, and awards —as more than an 
occasion for reminiscence and reconnection.  This annual event, which will continue to be celebrated long into the future, 
stands as a powerful testament to the community’s enduring resilience and unity in the face of change; now, therefore:

SECTION 2. The Council celebrates the extraordinary contributions of the residents, past and present, of this historic

CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 20, 2025 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing the World Day of Remembrance for Road Traffic Victims and affirming the 
city’s commitment to Vision Zero Indianapolis. 
 
WHEREAS, traffic violence claimed the lives of more than 643 Indianapolis residents between 2019 and 2023, 
according to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA); and 
 
WHEREAS, in 2024, there were 101 fatalities for people driving or riding in a motor vehicle, 33 fatalities for 
people walking, and 4 fatalities for people riding bikes in the City of Indianapolis, according to the Indiana State Police 
Fatal Crash Reporting System; and 
 
WHEREAS, this resolution recognizes that in the first ten months of 2025, there have been 67 fatalities for people 
driving or riding in a motor vehicle, 18 fatalities for people walking, and 9 fatalities for people riding bikes. The 9 
fatalities for people riding bikes is a new record with two months remaining in the year; and 
 
WHEREAS, this resolution memorializes those who have lost their lives in traffic crashes, elevates awareness of 
the urgent need for bold and systematic action to make our roadways safe for everyone, and aligns with both the global 
campaign of the World Day of Reme mbrance for Road Traffic Victims and the City of Indianapolis’ Vision Zero 
commitment; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Vision Zero Indianapolis initiative sets a goal to eliminate all traffic-related fatalities and serious 
injuries on city streets by the year 2035, through a data -driven, equitable Safe Systems Approach that prioritizes safe 
street design, speed management, and community engagement; and

who have been killed or seriously injured on the world’s roads, and acknowledging the profound suffering of victims,

• Remember all those killed or seriously injured on the roads; 
• Acknowledge the crucial work of emergency responders, medical professionals, and advocates; and 
• Promote evidence-based actions to prevent and ultimately eliminate future road traffic deaths and injuries;

WHEREAS, the 2025 World Day of Remembrance theme, “Remember. Support. Act.”, reminds all levels of 
government and the public of our shared responsibility to increase safety measures and take meaningful action to protect

Proposal No. 320, 2025 was retitled GENERAL ORDINANCE NO. 39, 2025, and reads as follows: 
 
CITY-COUNTY GENERAL ORDINANCE NO. 39, 2025 
 
PROPOSAL FOR A GENERAL ORDINANCE to amend Chapter 283 of the Revised Code of the Consolidated City of 
Indianapolis – Marion County by modifying the duties and responsibilities of the Forensic Services Agency. 
 
BE IT ORDAINED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1. Section 283 -342 of the Revised Code of the Consolidated City and County, Indianapolis/Marion 
County, Indiana, is hereby amended by deleting the portions stricken through and adding the portions underlined to read 
as follows: 
 
Sec. 283-342.  Agency duties and responsibilities. 
 
(a) FSA shall be responsible for the supervision, management and operation of the county's criminal forensic 
scientific laboratory. 
 
(b) FSA shall provide subject law enforcement agencies with  comprehensive forensic laboratory services, 
including crime scene services, specialized evidentiary evaluations, examinations, scientific analyses, and database 
support. These services may cover a range of forensic disciplines related to evidence collection, material 
identification, comparative analyses, and authentication. FSA shall proactively assess the evolving needs of the 
public safety community and strive to adapt its offerings to effectively support the residents of the City of 
Indianapolis and Marion County. Additionally, scientific testing, identification evaluation, firearms identification, 
ballistics, authenticating documents, polygraph examinations, tool and die mark comparisons, photography, video 
and laser techniques, and evidence recovery. FSA shall furnish provide expert testimony upon request from in these 
areas if requested by subject agencies. 
 
SECTION 2. The expressed or implied repeal or amendment by this ordinance of any other ordinance or part of any other 
ordinance does not affect any rights or liabilities accrued, penalties incurred, or proceedings begun prior to the effective 
date of this ordinance. Those rights, liabilities, and proceedings are continued, and penalties shall be imposed and 
enforced under the repealed or amended ordinance as if this ordinance had not been adopted.
