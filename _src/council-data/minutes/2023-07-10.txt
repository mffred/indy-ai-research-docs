CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 28, 2023 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing Moms Demand Action and Students Demand Action as two organizations that 
have demonstrated an unwavering commitment to advocating for common-sense gun legislation and ending the epidemic 
of gun violence in our communities. 
 
 WHEREAS, following the Sandy Hook tragedy in 2012, where a gunman entered an elementary school and took 
the lives of twenty children and six educators, Shannon Watts founded Moms Demand Action as a grassroots movement 
to empower and mobilize individuals to fight for stronger gun laws and safer communities; and 
 
 WHEREAS, with chapters in every state and more than 10 million supporters, Moms Demand Action, through its 
advocacy efforts, has been instrumental in securing important victories at the state and federal levels, including the 
passage of red flag laws and expanding background checks; and  
 
 WHEREAS, Students Demand Action is a student-led initiative that is part of Moms Demand Action and works to 
empower young people to take action on gun violence prevention; and   
 
WHEREAS, both Moms Demand Action and Students Demand Action have successfully organized rallies and 
events across the country and have been a powerful voice in the fight for gun reform; and 
 
WHEREAS, estimates show that 4.6 million American children live in a home where at least one gun is kept loaded 
and unlocked; and 
 
WHEREAS, today’s youth have grown up with lockdown drills and safety seminars,  resulting in lifelong 
psychological trauma; 
 
 WHEREAS, guns are the leading cause of death among children, teens, and college-aged youth in the United States; 
and  
 
 WHEREAS, in 2022 alone, 43,000 students were exposed to an active shooter event, in which a total of 34 people, 
including children and adults, died from gunshot wounds; and   
 
 WHEREAS, the tireless efforts of Moms Demand Action and Students Demand Action have helped shine a light 
on the devastating toll that gun violence takes on our communities, and, by speaking out and demanding action, these 
organizations have inspired others to get involved in protecting their communities; and   
 
 WHEREAS, given the rising trend in gun-related deaths in Indiana and the current state laws that allow such deadly 
weapons to proliferate unimpeded, the Indiana chapters of Moms Demand Action and Students Demand Action play a 
critical role in advocating for legislative reform to ensure that young Hoosiers are secure in their homes and their schools; 
Now, therefore, 
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1. The Indianapolis City-County Council recognizes and commends Moms Demand Action and Students 
Demand Action for their outstanding work and advocacy efforts to end gun violence in our communities. 
 
SECTION 2. The Council extends its deepest gratitude to Moms Demand Action and Students Demand Action for 
their tireless efforts to effect change and their continued pursuit of a future free from gun violence. 
 
SECTION 3. The Mayor is invited to join in this resolution by affixing his signature hereto. 
 
SECTION 4. This resolution shall be in full force and effect upon adoption and compliance with IC 36-3-4-14.

CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 32, 2023 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing Mayor Joe Hogsett and the Indianapolis City-County Council’s commitment 
to investment in Marion County’s infrastructure, despite state road funding disparities. 
 
WHEREAS, Marion County operates as a donor county, contributing more in taxes to the state than it receives back 
for road funding; and 
 
WHEREAS, Indianapolis residents have suffered from decades of underinvestment in Marion County’s local roads, 
highly traveled thoroughfares, and bridges, as a result of state road funding that is allocated based on how long a road is, 
without regard to how wide or frequently traveled that road happens to be; and 
 
WHEREAS, despite state road funding disparities, Mayor Joe Hogsett and the Indianapolis City-County Council 
have made historic investments in Marion County’s infrastructure, working together to overcome a structural budget 
deficit, add $30 million in new public works equipment, increase the transportation budget from $78 million in 2017 to 
$252 million in 2023, and invest more than $100 million in long-overlooked residential streets without adding additional 
burden to Marion County taxpayers; and 
 
WHEREAS, Mayor Hogsett has worked with the Indiana General Assembly to adjust the current state road funding 
formula to better represent Marion County’s population and lay groundwork for future conversations involving the state 
road funding formula’s impact on Marion County’s infrastructure; and 
 
WHEREAS, Marion County and its infrastructure play a critical role in the economic health and cultural landscape 
of Central Indiana, supporting not only the residents of Indianapolis, but the region as a whole. Inaction on infrastructure 
risks falling behind those with whom we compete for students, talent, jobs, and residents; and

CITY-COUNTY GENERAL ORDINANCE NO. 34, 2023 
 
A PROPOSAL FOR A GENERAL ORDINANCE to add additional gun safety measures to Revised Code Chapter 451,

BE IT ORDAINED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1.  Title III, Chapter 451 of the "Revised Code of the Consolidated City and County," regarding public health and 
welfare, hereby is amended add the following section: 
 
Sec. 451-7. – Gun Safety Measures (Conditional). 
 
(a) The Council recognizes that the provisions of subsections (b) through (e) below are currently unenforceable under 
the Indiana statute restricting most forms of gun regulation by local governments, Ind. Code Ch. 35-47-11.1, and by other 
relevant provisions of state law. Therefore, subsections (b) through (e) below shall not take effect, and shall not be enforced, 
unless and until the Corporation Counsel certifies in a written notice to the President of the Council that all or part of 
subsections (b) through (e) have become enforceable and are not preempted by or in conflict with the United States 
Constitution, the Indiana Constitution, federal statute, and/or Indiana statute. The written notice from the Corporation Counsel 
shall specify the measure(s) herein that have become enforceable, and such measure(s) shall take effect only after ninety (90)

measure(s). 
 
(b) Notwithstanding any other provision of this chapter or the Revised Code, it shall be unlawful in Marion County to 
buy, sell, possess, display, carry, brandish, or discharge a semiautomatic assault weapon, as that term is defined in the proposed 
federal Assault Weapons Ban of 2023 (S.25), Section 2(a), as introduced in the United States Senate on January 23, 2023.  
 
(c) It shall be unlawful in Marion County for a person under twenty-one (21) years of age to purchase a firearm. It shall 
be unlawful in Marion County to sell a firearm to a person under twenty-one (21) years of age. As used in this section, the

(e) Notwithstanding subsection (d) above, no person shall carry a concealed firearm in Marion County. As used in this

CITY-COUNTY GENERAL ORDINANCE NO. 36, 2023 
 
PROPOSAL FOR A GENERAL ORDINANCE to amend the Revised Code to allow for the establishment of greenway 
crossing speed limit zones. 
 
BE IT ORDAINED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
 CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1.  Chapter 441 of the Revised Code of the Consolidated City and County, regarding Traffic, specifically Article 
III, Division 2 regarding Speed, be and is hereby amended by the addition of a new section to include the language that is 
underscored as follows: 
 
Sec. 441-326. – Greenway crossing zones. 
 
(a)  There is hereby created within the consolidated city areas defined and established as greenway crossing zones, which 
shall be defined as that portion of any street or highway contiguous to any at-grade crossing of a greenway with the street or 
highway.  The dimensions of said zone at each greenway crossing shall vary depending on the posted speed limit along the 
street or highway in each direction from the crossing, amount of traffic on the street or highway in the vicinity of the crossing, 
amount of traffic on the greenway trail in the vicinity of the crossing, roadway and greenway trail geometrics in the area, and 
any other criteria as determined by the engineering division of the department of public works. 
 
(b)   The engineering division of the department of public works, following an engineering and traffic investigation and 
based on the factors set forth in subsection (a), shall determine an appropriate speed limit for each greenway crossing zone 
established pursuant to subsection (a). 
 
(c) No person shall drive a motor vehicle on any street or highway within a greenway crossing zone established pursuant 
to subsection (a) at a speed greater than the speed limit prescribed pursuant to subsection (b). 
 
(d)  No restriction under this section shall be a pplicable until the department of public works posts a notice of its 
determination of a speed limit for the greenway crossing zone on its website at least thirty (30) days before posting signage or 
other traffic control devices, posts reasonable and adequate signs and/or other traffic control devices indicating where such 
greenway crossing zones begin, the reduced speed limit within the zone, and where such zones end or the speed limit for the 
section of street or highway that follows.  
 
(e) Where no special hazard exists, the speed limit prescribed for a greenway crossing zone pursuant to subsection (b) 
shall be lawful, but any speed in excess of that limit shall be prima facie evidence that the speed is not reasonable or prudent 
and is unlawful.  When a special hazard does exist, the driver of a motor vehicle shall reduce their speed such that is reasonable 
and prudent under the conditions, having regard to the actual and potential hazards then existing, the posted speed limit 
notwithstanding.   For purposes of this subsection, a special hazard shall exist when users of a greenway trail are within the 
crossing of a street or highway or are adjacent thereto awaiting an opportunity to cross the street or highway.  
 
SECTION 2. The expressed or implied repeal or amendment by this ordinance of any other ordinance or part  of any other 
ordinance does not affect any rights or liabilities accrued, penalties incurred, or proceedings begun prior to the effective 
date of this ordinance. Those rights, liabilities, and proceedings are continued, and penalties shall be imposed and enforced 
under the repealed or amended ordinance as if this ordinance had not been adopted. 
 
SECTION 3. Should any provision (section, paragraph, sentence, clause, or any other portion) of this  ordinance be 
declared by a court of competent jurisdiction to be invalid for any reason, the remaining provision or provisions shall 
not be affected, if and only if such remaining provisions can, without the invalid provision or provisions, be given the 
effect intended by the Council in adopting this ordinance. To this end the provisions of this ordinance are severable. 
 
SECTION 4. This ordinance shall be in effect from and after its passage by the Council and compliance with Ind. Code §

CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL ORDINANCE NO. 10, 2023 
 
PROPOSAL FOR A SPECIAL ORDINANCE approving an interlocal cooperation agreement between the Consolidated City 
of Indianapolis and Marion County, Indiana (“Indianapolis”) and the Town of Avon, Indiana (“Avon”) for the construction 
and operation of improvements to Raceway Road. 
 
WHEREAS, I.C. § 8 -17-1-45 provides that each county is responsible for the construction, reconstruction, 
maintenance, and operation  of the roads, including the ditches and signs for those roads, making up its southern and 
eastern boundaries; and 
 
WHEREAS, I.C. 36 -1-3-9(a) provides that municipalities have exclusive jurisdiction over the bridges, streets, 
alleys, sidewalks, watercourses, sewers, drains, and public grounds inside its corporate boundaries; and  
 
WHEREAS, Raceway Road is the boundary road between Marion County and Hendricks County, and it is the 
eastern boundary of Hendricks County; therefore, Avon is responsible for the construction, reconstruction, maintenance, 
and operation of the segment of Raceway Road within its boundaries; and 
 
WHEREAS, Avon is preparing plans and specifications for a capital improvement project to improve the intersection of 
Raceway Road and County Road 100 North, which is West 10
th Street in Indianapolis (the “Project”), and the project will 
require Avon to acquire right-of-way and to conduct construction activities within the boundaries of Indianapolis; and 
 
WHEREAS, pursuant to I.C. § 36-1-4-5, municipalities have the corporate power to acquire and own interests in real 
property and, pursuant to I.C. § 36-1-4-6, to use and improve interests in real property; and  
 
WHEREAS, pursuant to I.C. §§ 36-1-4-18 and 36-1-3-9(c), a municipality may exercise its corporate powers outside its 
boundaries and inside the boundaries of another municipality if the two entities enter into an interlocal cooperation agreement 
approved by the fiscal body of each municipality under I.C. § 36-1-7-2; and 
 
WHEREAS, accordingly, Avon and Indianapolis desire to enter into an interlocal cooperation agreement to allow Avon 
to carry out the Project and to operate and maintain the Project improvements within the boundaries of Indianapolis; and 
 
WHEREAS, the City-County Council, having considered the matter and being duly advised, finds that the intersection 
or Raceway Road and West 10
th Street (CR 100 N) serves and affects the citizens of and the economic well-being of both 
Indianapolis and Avon, that the Project will improve safety and mobility, and will accommodate future economic growth in 
the region, and that it will be of public utility and benefit to enter into an interlocal cooperation agreement with Avon to carry 
out the Project; now, therefore: 
 
BE IT ORDAINED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1.  The City-County Council hereby approves an authorizes the Department of Public Works to enter into an 
interlocal cooperation agreement with the Town of Avon for purposes of authorizing Avon to acquire right-of-way, to operate 
and maintain such right-of-way, and to conduct construction activities within the boundaries of Indianapolis as necessary for 
completion of the Project to improve the intersection of Raceway Road and West 10th Street (County Road 100 North in Avon) 
and for the continued operation and maintenance of the improvements to said intersection.
