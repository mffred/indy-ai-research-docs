CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 16, 2021 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing the service of teachers and educational staff throughout the COVID -19 
pandemic and urging policy makers to prioritize vaccinations for these workers. 
 
WHEREAS, the United States public education system plays a un ique and critical role in the functioning of our 
community, our society, and our democracy; and 
 
WHEREAS, that system is facing unprecedented challenges due to the global coronavirus pandemic, including 
critical shortages of full-time and substitute teachers due to COVID-19 exposure, illness, and even death; and 
 
WHEREAS, in November of 2020 the Indianapolis Star reported that across central Indiana staffing shortages were 
so dire in some schools that administrators were having to choose between closing sch ool buildings or asking teachers 
who had been exposed to COVID-19 to keep working without the recommended quarantine period, while in virtually all 
schools teachers have been asked to take on more classes and duties; and 
 
WHEREAS, throughout the pandemic, K-12 teachers, administrators, media specialists, school social workers, 
coaches, bus drivers, cafeteria workers, custodians, and all manner of educational support staff have functioned as 
essential workers, returning to work after COVID shutdowns long bef ore many other professions and often without the 
necessary PPE or procedures in place to ensure their safety; and 
 
WHEREAS, the AARP reported in 2020 that more than 70% of our nation’s 370,000 school bus drivers are over 55 
years old, making them more vulnerable to the coronavirus; and whereas these bus drivers spend hours every work day 
in an enclosed spaced with children of all ages; and 
 
WHEREAS, school custodians, cafeteria workers, and bus drivers face some of the greatest risks for virus exposure 
among all educational workers but are typically paid much less for their work and often lack health insurance, while at 
the same time the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reports these jobs employ high concentrations of Black and Hispanic 
workers, groups known to be disproportionately impacted by COVID-19; and 
 
WHEREAS, researchers at Education Week reported that as of February 9, 2021, 740 active and retired K -12 
educators and personnel have died of COVID-19, a number that doesn’t include complete counts from districts that don't 
share data, educators who have survived with lingering COVID-related health issues, or loved ones who caught the virus 
from them and died; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Indiana State Department of Health reports that since the start of the pandemic , nearly 5,500 
teachers and another 6,300 school staff have tested positive for COVID-19, a figure that also does not include Indiana’s 
290 K-12 schools who have chosen not to report cases to the State; and  
 
WHEREAS, the Kaiser Family Foundation  reported last year that one in four U.S. teachers -- nearly 1.5 million 
educators -- have preexisting conditions that increase their risk of serious illness from Covid -19, while countless others 
live with immunocompromised loved ones; and

WHEREAS, the Centers for Disease Control & Prevention recommends that “teachers, support staff, and daycare 
workers” be considered frontline essential workers and included Phase 1B of state’s vaccination plans; and 
 
WHEREAS, Indiana entered Phase 1B on February 1, 2021 but three d ays later the Indianapolis Star reported that 
“There is still no timeline on when Indiana's teachers will be eligible for the COVID -19 vaccine, even as state officials 
push schools to offer more in -person instruction and surrounding states have begun vacci nating their own educator 
workforces”; and  
 
WHEREAS, as of February 11, 2021, at least 28 states, the District of Columbia, and Puerto Rico have made some 
or all teachers eligible to receive the coronavirus vaccine, and Indiana’s neighboring states of Ill inois, Michigan, Ohio 
and Kentucky are currently vaccinating teachers; now, therefore: 
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1. The City -County Council calls on Indiana’s policy maker s to prioritize COVID -19 vaccination of K -12 
educators and staff as soon as sufficient vaccine supply is available. 
 
SECTION 2. The Council likewise urges our partners in public health to carefully consider the age and racial makeup of 
education workers to ensure equitable distribution of the COVID-19 vaccine. 
 
SECTION 3. The Council encourages Indiana’s education leaders to provide K-12 schools with the resources to provide 
onsite COVID testing for teachers and school staff to ensure accurate reporting of school infection rates and support 
infection control measures.  
 
SECTION 4. Until those resources become available, the Council urges Marion County’s K -12 teachers and staff to 
regularly access community-based COVID testing to measure and mitigate the spread of COVID-19 in school settings.  
 
SECTION 5. The Mayor is invited to join in this resolution by affixing his signature hereto. 
 
SECTION 6.  This resolution shall be in full force and effect upon adoption and compliance with IC 36-3-4-14.

Sec. 231-507. - Obstruction of Public Right of Way Prohibited.  
 
Notwithstanding the other provisions of this Article, neither camping nor storing personal property is 
permitted in a manner that wholly or partially obstructs pedestrian traffic on any sidewalk, street, or other public right 
of way. A sidewalk, street, or  other public right of way is partially obstructed if: (1) more than one half of its width is 
blocked at any point, (2) the normal flow of pedestrians or vehicles is slowed or subject to a bottleneck, or (3) 
pedestrians are required to step onto a street o r otherwise expose themselves to danger in order to pass around the 
camp or stored personal property.  
 
The city shall give persons who are camping or storing personal property in a manner that partially or wholly 
obstructs a public right of way the option  of rearranging their camp or personal property such that it does not wholly 
or partially obstruct the public right of way. If it is not possible for the camp or stored personal property to be in the 
area without wholly or partially obstructing the right o f way, or if the person camping or storing personal property 
has been given at least one opportunity to remedy the obstruction and has failed or refused to do so, then the person 
may be required to cease camping in the area and to remove his or her persona l property.
