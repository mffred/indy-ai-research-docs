CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 13, 2023 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing John Leslie (Wes) Montgomery.    
 
 WHEREAS, John Leslie (Wes) Montgomery, world famous Jazz guitarist and composer, was born in Indianapolis, 
Indiana on March 6, 1923, and passed away June 15, 1968; and 
 
 WHEREAS, Wes, widely regarded as the greatest jazz guitarist of all time, was a self-taught musical prodigy who 
was renowned for his unique technique of plucking the strings with the side of his thumb and for his extensive use of 
octaves, which gave him a unique sound and set him apart from every other guitarist; and nonetheless, his ability to play 
anyplace with his instrument was made possible by his inability to read or write music; and  
 
 WHEREAS, not being able to read or write music freed him from being constrained by conventional playing 
techniques and allowed him to perform his instrument anyplace. Wes developed a new method for playing the instrument 
and rose to the top of every musical genre.  Wes revolutionized jazz forever by rising to become a well -known jazz 
guitarist; and 
 
     WHEREAS, because of his distinctive style, Wes—who has been dubbed the "Father of Smooth Jazz"—became one 
of the most significant figures in introducing newcomers to jazz. His soft tones and capacity to personalize any song are 
praised by musicians around. Distinguished Professor of Music and Chairman of the Jazz Department at Indiana 
University's Jacobs School of Music, Dr. David Nathaniel was quoted as saying “He ( Wes) was as great a teacher as he 
was a player”; and  
 
 WHEREAS, in addition to winning Grammys for "Going out of my head" (1966), "Willow weep for me" (1969), 
the Grammy Hall of Fame Award, and "The Incredible Jazz Guitar of Wes Montgomery" (1988), Wes also garnered

"A day in the life"; and Wes Montgomery is now a representation of the greatness and brightness of Indiana history and

BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1. The Indianapolis City-County Council recognizes world-famous jazz guitarist John Leslie (W es)

CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 15, 2023 
 
A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing the grand opening of Hannah’s Memorial Playground. 
 
 WHEREAS, in September, 2021, seven-year-old first grader Hannah Crutchfield was taken from us in a tragic car 
crash at George W. Julian School 57 in Irvington that injured her mother and a George Julian crossing guard; and   
 
 WHEREAS, George Julian students created the idea to  revamp the playground in Hannah’s honor. With support 
from parents, school families, faculty, and the PTSA, the George Julian family embarked on a playground project to 
serve children from kindergarten through 8th grade; and 
 
 WHEREAS, a massive community fundraising effort began, ultimately raising approximately $240,000 from local 
businesses, charities, labor, foundations and more than 740 individuals to cover the cost of new equipment, several 
murals, and clean-up of the former playground areas; and  
 
 WHEREAS, at the grand opening and ribbon-cutting celebration for Hannah’s Memorial Playground on Saturday, 
February 25, 2023, Hannah’s mother, Cassandra Crutchfield, expressed how brave and adventurous her daughter was, 
and what a loving and playful spirit she had; and 
 
 WHEREAS, Hannah’s Memorial Playground introduces new ways for children to play on equipment that brings all 
students together in a safe and welcoming space, allowing George Julian students a place to process the pai n and grief 
associated with the loss of their special friend; and  
 
 WHEREAS, Hannah’s Memorial Playground at George Julian is now open to the entire community, where 
generations of Irvington children and families gather to play on the playground, enjoy the basketball courts, or harvest 
vegetables from the school’s community garden; and   
 
 WHEREAS, the playground serves as a legacy to Hannah Crutchfield, a vibrant space that will inspire students and 
their Irvington neighbors for years to come. It is also a testament of how the George Julian family, the Indianapolis Public 
Schools community, and the people of the Eastside will always hold Hannah and her family in our hearts; now, therefore: 
 
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1. The Indianapolis City-County Council recognizes the grand opening of Hannah’s Memorial Playground 
in Irvington, created in memory of seven-year-old first grader Hannah Crutchfield. 
 
SECTION 2. The Mayor is invited to join in this resolution by affixing his signature hereto. 
 
SECTION 3. This resolution shall be in full force and effect upon adoption and compliance with IC 36-3-4-14.

CITY-COUNTY GENERAL ORDINANCE NO. 6, 2023 
 
PROPOSAL FOR A GENERAL ORDINANCE to amend the Revised Code to clarify procedures for improvement location 
permits related to signs.  
 
BE IT ORDAINED BY THE CITY-COUNTY COUNCIL OF THE 
CITY OF INDIANAPOLIS AND OF MARION COUNTY, INDIANA: 
 
SECTION 1.  Chapter 226, Article III of the "Revised Code of the Consolidated City and County," regarding the department 
of business and neighborhood services, division of administration, logistics, licenses and permits, is hereby amended by the 
insertion of new text in Section 226-304 (previously reserved), to read as follows: 
 
Sec. 226-304. Procedure for signs. 
 
Notwithstanding any other provision of Chapter 740, Article VIII of the Revised Code, the following provisions 
apply where an improvement location permit is sought for the location, erection, alteration, or repair of a sign (as that 
term is defined by Section 740-202 of the Revised Code): 
 
A. The Administrator or the Administrator’s designee shall provide an initial response to an application for permit 
no later than twelve (12) business days after the application is received by the Administrator. An application 
will be considered received upon being properly submitted in the manner prescribed by the Administrator. An 
initial response is defined as one of the following: 
 
1. the issuance of the permit;  
2. the denial of the permit application; or  
3. a written response to the applicant stating (a) that the application is incomplete because it lacks 
information required by Section 740- 802 and (b) that review will be completed  only after such 
missing required information is supplied. 
 
B. If the Administrator or the Administrator’s designee timely provides an initial response under subparagraph 
(A)(3) above, the Administrator or the Administrator’s designee shall subsequently issue  a final response, 
constituting either the issuance of the permit or the denial of the application, no later than five (5) business days 
after the Administrator receives from the applicant the additional information necessary to make the permit 
application complete. 
 
C. Except as provided in paragraph (D) below, the permit is deemed issued if all applicable permit application 
fees have been paid in full and the Administrator or the Administrator’s designee does not provide an initial 
response within the time period (twelve (12) business days) required by paragraph (A) above or a final response 
within the time period (five (5) business days) required by paragraph (B) above. 
 
D. The permit shall not be deemed as issued, regardless of the amount of time elapsed since the application is 
received by the Administrator, if the Administrator or the Administrator’s designee timely provides an initial 
response under subparagraph (A)(3) above and the applicant does not subsequently supply the missing required 
information. 
 
E. A permit that is deemed issued under paragraph (C) above is subject to automatic revocation under Section 
740-809 and revocation in event of violation under Section 740- 810, on the same basis as if a permit were 
actually issued. A sign that is located, erect ed, altered, or repaired pursuant to a permit deemed issued under 
paragraph (C) remains subject to all requirements of the zoning ordinance that pertain to signs, and the 
enforcement provisions of Article X of Chapter  740 apply to the location, erection, alteration, or repair of a 
sign that is deemed permitted on the same basis as if a permit were actually issued. For the avoidance of doubt, 
a deemed-issued permit obviates the permit requirement under Section 744 -903 but does not legally establish

740.”

SECTION 1.  Sec. 836-1 of the "Revised Code of the Consolidated City and County," is hereby amended by adding the 
language that is underlined to read as follows: 
 
Sec. 836-1. Definitions. 
 
As used in this chapter, the following terms shall have the meanings ascribed to them in this section:  
 
Animal rescue organization means a not-for-profit organization having tax-exempt status under 501(c)(3) of the United 
States Internal Revenue Code, whose mission and practice is, in whole or in significant part, the rescue and placement of 
animals into permanent homes. The term animal rescue organization does not include any person that: (1) breeds animals for 
profit; (2) is located on the same premises as a person that breeds animals for profit; (3) has any personnel in common with a 
person that breeds animals for profit; (4) obtains, in exchange for payment or compensation, dogs or cats from a person that 
breeds animals for profit; or (5) facilitates the sale of dogs or cats that were obtained, in exchange for payment or compensation, 
from a person that breeds animals for profit. 
 
Cat means any member of the species Felis catus.  
 
Dog means any member of the species Canis familiaris.  
 
Kennel means a facility operated commercially and principally for the purpose of boarding, housing, grooming, 
breeding or training dogs or cats, or both; a kennel, for purposes of this chapter, shall not include a facility in or adjoining a 
private residence where dogs are kept for the hobby of the householder using them for hunting, practice tracking, exhibiting 
in shows or field or obedience trials or for the guarding or protecting of the householder's property, and an occasional sale of 
puppies or kittens by the owner, lessee or other occupant of such property shall not make that property a kennel for the purposes 
of this chapter.  
 
Offer for sale means to proffer for acceptance by another person.  
 
Person shall mean an individual, corporation, partnership, association, or any other entity. 
 
Pet shop means: (1) a facility operated commercially and principally for the purpose of selling animals which, in the 
hands of their immediate purchasers, will be pets; or (2) any retail store where animals are kept, sold, or offered for sale on the 
premises.  An animal care services shelter, as described in section 226-512 of this Code, or an animal rescue organization, as 
defined in this section, shall not be considered a pet shop. 
 
Rabbit means any member of the species Oryctolagus cuniculus domesticus.  
 
Sell means to exchange for consideration, adopt out, barter, auction, trade, lease, or otherwise transfer an animal.

SECTION 2.  Chapter 836 of the "Revised Code of the Consolidated City and County" is hereby amended to add Sec. 836-6, 
“Prohibition on the Retail Sale of Dogs, Cats, or Rabbits by Pet Shops,” reading as follows: 
 
Sec. 836-6. - Prohibition on the Retail Sale of Dogs, Cats, or Rabbits by Pet Shops 
 
(a) It shall be unlawful for a pet shop to sell or offer for sale a dog, cat, or rabbit unless the animal was obtained by 
the pet shop from either an animal services shelter described in section 226 -512 of this Code or an animal rescu e 
organization, as defined in section 836-1 and all of the following conditions are met: 
 
(1) All fees or other charges for the sale of a dog, cat or rabbit shall not exceed $500 per animal; and 
 
(2) The pet shop posts in a conspicuous location affixed to the enclosure of each dog, cat or rabbit available for 
sale: the name of and city and state for the animal services shelter or animal rescue organization from which 
the pet store obtained the dog, cat or rabbit. 
 
(b) Maintenance of Records.  
 
(1) A pet shop shall maintain records documenting from which animal services shelter or animal rescue 
organization the pet shop obtained each dog, cat or rabbit in the possession of the pet store for not less than one 
year following the date the pet store takes possession of the dog, cat or rabbit. 
 
(2) A pet shop shall make the records maintained under this section available for inspection upon request by the 
department of business and neighborhood services or its designee.  
 
(c) Enforcement and penalties. 
 
(1) This chapter shall be enforced by the department of business and neighborhood services or its designee.  
 
(2) A pet shop that sells or offers for sale a dog, cat, or rabbit in violation of subsection 836- 6(a) is subject to 
admission of violation and payment of a civil penalty in an amount provided in section 103-52 of the Code for 
the pet shop’s first violation in a twelve -month period. All second and subsequent violations in any twelve -
month period are subject to the enforcement procedures and penalties provided in  section 103-3 of this Code, 
and the fine imposed for any subsequent violation in any twelve -month period shall not be less than seven 
hundred fifty dollars ($750.00). 
 
(3) A pet shop that fails to maintain and provide records in violation of subsection 836-6(b) is subject to admission 
of violation and payment of a civil penalty in an amount provided in section 10 3-52 of the Code for the pet 
shop’s first violation in a twelve -month period. All second and subsequent violations in any twelve -month 
period are subject to the enforcement procedures and penalties provided in section 103-3 of this Code, and the 
fine imposed for any subsequent violation in any twelve -month period shall not be less than seven hundred 
fifty dollars ($750.00). 
 
(4) Any violation under this chapter shall be reported to the license administrator and subject to  action under 
Chapter 801 of this Code.  
 
(d) Effective date. Section 836-6 shall take effect on May 1, 2025 for any pet shop registered with the administrator 
prior to April 1, 2023 the following dates:  
 
(1) For pet shops in operation after September 1, 2021, Sec. 836-6 shall take effect on May 1, 2023.  
(2) For pet shops in operation before September 1, 2021, Sec. 836-6 shall take effect on May 1, 2025.
