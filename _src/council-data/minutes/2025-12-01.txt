CITY-COUNTY SPECIAL RESOLUTION NO. 21, 2025 
 
PROPOSAL FOR A SPECIAL RESOLUTION recognizing and honoring Steven Quick for his extraordinary career, his 
decades of service to the City of Indianapolis, and his lasting impact on the community. 
 
WHEREAS, Steven Quick was born on February 19, 1959, in Indianapolis, Indiana, and is a proud graduate of 
Emmerich Manuel High School, Class of 1978; and 
 
WHEREAS, in 1988, Steven married his beloved wife, Leslie, and together they have built a strong and loving 
family that includes three daughters, two sons, twenty grandchildren, and three great-grandchildren; and 
 
WHEREAS, Steven began his career with the Department of Transportation in January 1984 as a laborer, quickly 
advancing through the ranks due to his strong work ethic and dedication to public service, ultimately becoming a Heavy 
Equipment Operator in 1987; and 
 
WHEREAS, Steven’s leadership and commitment to his fellow workers led to his election as Vice President of 
AFSCME Local 725 in 1993, and just one year later, to his election as President—a position he has held with distinction 
for over 30 years, most recently reaffirmed by his re-election in July 2025; and 
 
WHEREAS, Steven has consistently demonstrated a deep commitment to community service, contributing his time 
and talents to numerous boards, school programs, and nonprofit organizations throughout Indianapolis; and 
 
WHEREAS, throughout his 41-year career with the Department of Public Works, Steven has been a vital partner in 
advancing the City’s mission, playing a key role in the collaborative model established in the early 1990s under Mayor 
Stephen Goldsmith and the leadership of Charles Snyder; and continuing through the Peterson, Ballard and Hogsett 
administrations; and 
 
WHEREAS, Steven has been a very trusted partner of the City-County Council and has worked collaboratively with 
its many members through these years to support priorities of the city’s constituents; and  
 
WHEREAS, Steven Quick’s legacy is one of leadership, service, and unwavering dedication to the betterment of

WHEREAS, the Indianapolis Recorder Newspaper, founded in 1895 by visionary publisher George P. Stewart, has 
served as a steadfast voice for Indiana’s Black community for 130 years, making it one of the longest -running African 
American newspapers in the United States and a cornerstone of Indiana’s journalistic and cultural landscape; and 
 
WHEREAS, born during an era when mainstream media often excluded or misrepresented Black voices, the 
Recorder was established to inform, uplift, and advocate for African Americans in Indianapolis and across the state, 
providing essential coverage of civil rights, education, business, faith, and everyday life from the Black perspective; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Recorder’s  historic stewardship has passed through the dedicated leadership of notable owners 
including Marcus C. Stewart, Eunice Trotter, and William G. Mays, each of whom made lasting contributions to 
preserving the publication's integrity and extending its impact; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Recorder  has also benefited from the leadership of distinguished past presidents Charles Blair, 
Carolene Mays, and Shannon Williams, who played key roles in guiding the publication through critical times of 
transition, growth, and innovation; and 
 
WHEREAS, throughout its long and distinguished history, the Indianapolis Recorder has chronicled both local and 
national movements—from the Great Migration and Jim Crow era to the Civil Rights Movement, modern -day calls for 
police reform, and today’s ongoing struggles for social, racial, and economic justice; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Recorder has remained resilient through wars, economic downturns, and shifting media landscapes, 
evolving with the times by embracing digital platforms, expanding its multimedia reach, and serving as a training ground 
for Black journalists and storytellers committed to truth and equity; and 
 
WHEREAS, in addition to its award-winning journalism, the Recorder has created a profound civic impact through 
programs and initiatives that engage the community, including voter registration drives, public health education, youth 
empowerment projects, and economic development partnerships; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Recorder’s commitment to journalistic excellence has been recognized nationally and locally, 
having earned over 100 awards in the past six years alone from esteemed institutions such as the Hoosier State Press 
Association, the Society of Professional Journalists, and the National Newspaper Publishers Association; and 
 
WHEREAS, the newspaper’s signature Champions of Diversity Awards, now in its 18th year, has grown to become 
one of Indiana’s largest annual celebrations of equity and inclusion, honoring individuals and institutions that are creating 
lasting change in business, education, healthcare, and civic life; and 
 
WHEREAS, under the current leadership of Robert Shegog, President and CEO of Recorder Media Group—which 
includes the Indianapolis Recorder Newspaper and Indiana Minority Business Magazine —the Recorder continues to 
thrive as a multimedia force through its affiliated platforms, while investing in investigative journalism, community 
engagement, and content that preserves and protects Black history; and 
 
WHEREAS, the paper’s enduring motto—“Our News, Your Voice” —embodies its commitment to inclusive 
storytelling, cultural pride, and speaking truth to power, especially on behalf of underrepresented and marginalized 
communities; and

just as a publication but as a movement—one that continues to shape the conscience of our city and state through fearless

WHEREAS, Alpha Kappa Alpha's diverse, collaborative work throughout the United States, the Caribbean, Europe, 
Africa, and United Arab Emirates integrates a high level of personal engagement with the strong bonds of sisterhood and

All definitions in IC 7.1 -3-31 et seq., as amended, including, but not limited to, Designated Outdoor 
Refreshment Area, Craft Manufacturer, Designated Permittee, Licensed Premises, Retailer Permittee, and

Sec. 820-102. EFFECT OF CHAPTER. 
 
(a) This chapter is applicable to Retailer Permittees and Vendor s who wish to participate in any 
DORA established by the city and any person(s) who consume or wish to consume alcoholic 
beverages within a DORA. 
 
(b) Any Retailer Permittee or Vendor wishing to participate in any DORA established by the city 
shall abide by all state and local laws. 
 
(c) It shall be unlawful for any person, sole proprietor, partnership, company, corporation, 
applicant, vendor, restaurant, establishment, or venue to operate as a Designated Permittee or 
Vendor within a DORA unless registered as such with the city as provided in this chapter. 
 
Sec. 820-103. APPLICATION OF STATE AND LOCAL LAWS. 
 
(a) Any Designated Permittee shall follow any and all  other applicable state and local laws and 
regulations, not otherwise outlined herein, including, but not limited to, littering laws, noise 
nuisance laws, and alcoholic beverage consumption laws. 
 
(b) Any person, sole proprietor, partnership, company, corporation, applicant, vendor, restaurant, 
establishment, or venue who violates any state or local law may be fined accordingly. 
 
(c) In accordance with IC 7.1-3-31-11, Council may adopt an ordinance dissolving (or revoking) a 
DORA at any time. The Council shall notify the chair of the  Indiana Alcohol and T obacco 
Commission (ATC) that the DORA is dissolved. 
 
Sec. 820-104. INSURANCE AND PUBLIC LIABILITY. 
 
As a condition of registration an d applicant approval by the city, the city reserves the right to  require an 
owner or operator of an establishment wishing to participate as a Designated Permittee or Vendor to 
furnish annual certificates of insurance to the city  naming the Consolidated City of Indianapolis-Marion 
County as an additional insured, primary and non-contributory with coverages deemed to be acceptable 
to the city.

which may be amended from time to time.

containers of an alcoholic beverage at a time; and

be established by Council for each DORA approved by the ATC and the city.

(1) A map of the proposed DORA in sufficient detail to identify the boundaries;
