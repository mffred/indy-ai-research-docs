CITY-COUNTY FISCAL ORDINANCE NO. 1, 2021
Proposal No. 1, 2021

A FISCAL ORDINANCE amending the City-County Annual Budget for 2021 (City-County Fiscal Ordinance
No. 20, 2020) by additional appropriations of twelve million nine hundred sixteen thousand dollars ($12,916,000) for purposes of the City Non-Departmental.

WHEREAS, the COVID-19 pandemic has taken—and continues to take—a severe toll on our country, our state, and our Indianapolis and Marion County community; and

WHEREAS, the Centers for Disease Control (“CDC”) reports that COVID-19 may cause severe illness, including illness resulting in death, particularly among the elderly and those with underlying health conditions;
and

WHEREAS, Marion County has now experienced well over 72,000 confirmed cases of COVID-19 and well over 1,100 reported deaths confirmed to be caused by COVID-19; and

WHEREAS, the spread of COVID-19 throughout our country and our community poses a severe and imminent threat to public health and has required aggressive response measures to siow the spread of the disease and mitigate its impact; and

WHEREAS, the COVID-19 pandemic has also required, and continues to require, significant investments in local public-health efforts to mitigate the spread of coronavirus; and

WHEREAS, the COVID-19 pandemic has also required, and continues to require, significant investments to combat the secondary effects caused by the pandemic—including investments to mitigate significant pandemic-caused hardships being faced by Marion County's residents and small businesses; and

WHEREAS, City-County government previously established a significant residential rental assistance fund that is already preventing thousands of Marion County residents impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic from losing their homes; and

WHEREAS, demand for rental assistance has exceeded the capacity of that fund and requires further investment to avoid a wave of evictions in Marion County; and

WHEREAS, the secondary economic impacts from the COVID-19 pandemic have placed a significant burden on local food banks and non-profit food providers; and

WHEREAS, an investment in food assistance programming will help alleviate those burdens and ensure that those impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic have access to food; and

WHEREAS, rental and food assistance are important short-term measures to help Marion County residents impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic’s secondary economic effects, but iongerterm solutions require investment in training and education prograrnming for workers displaced from thelr jobs as a result of the pandemic; and

WHEREAS, our neighbors experiencing homelessness are at particularly acute risk during this pandemic due to their inability isolate themselves in a home and challenges accessing opportunities for food and hygiene in non-congregate settings; and

WHEREAS, an investment in services for those neighbors, including project management costs associated with temporary hotel housing, is necessary to combat COVID-19 within that population; and

WHEREAS, an expansion of the City-County’s existing Keys to Work / Pathways to Employment program provides another mechanism to transition our neighbors experiencing homelessness during the pandemic away from high-risk unhoused or congregate-living situations toward stable employment and housing; and

WHEREAS, IC § 10-14-3-17(j)(5)(H) provides that, in the event of a declared disaster emergency under
IC § 10-14-3-12, a political subdivision may “waive procedures and formalities otherwise required by law pertaining to .. . the appropriation and expenditure of public funds”; and
