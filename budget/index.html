Spending by function

The budget books group every agency into five functions. Public safety is IMPD, IFD, the Office of Public Health & Safety and 911. Criminal justice is the Sheriff, courts, prosecutor, public defender, Community Corrections, coroner and forensics. Community development & infrastructure covers DPW, Parks, DMD, Business & Neighborhood Services and Animal Care.

A = actual spending, AD = adopted, P = proposed. Not adjusted for inflation. 2020–2021 debt includes one-time debt and pension transactions, and 2022–2024 executive/administrative spending is inflated by one-time money run through those accounts, most likely federal pandemic relief (ARPA). Compare those years with care.

What changes from 2026 to 2027

Almost every function grows. The biggest jump by far is infrastructure, driven by the new state road program: $50M from the state, contingent on a $50M local match that has to grow every year through 2031. That match is the fight behind the wheel tax veto and override.

Where the money comes from

Local property and income taxes are more than half of revenue. Intergovernmental money (state gas tax, state road dollars, federal grants) is the swing factor: it spiked in 2022 with pandemic relief, dropped, and is climbing again with the state road match.

"Everything else" is charges for services, licenses and permits, fines, other taxes such as the wheel tax, investment earnings and other receipts. Investment earnings alone fell from $54M in 2024 to a projected $30M in 2027.

How to read a department

The city sorts all spending into five categories it calls "characters." Definitions below are from the budget book (page 55), in plain language.

Staff pay & benefitsCharacter 1 · Personal Services
Salaries and wages plus the employer's share of health and life insurance, pensions, unemployment and uniform allowances. For police and fire this includes pension payments.
SuppliesCharacter 2 · Materials and Supplies
Things that get used up or changed when used: office supplies, operating supplies, and repair and maintenance supplies.
Contracts, services & other chargesCharacter 3 · Other Services and Charges
Everything bought as a service: professional and consulting contracts, grants to outside groups, phones and transportation, printing and ads, insurance, utilities, contracted repairs, rent, and some debt payments.
Property & equipmentCharacter 4 · Capital
Buying or building lasting assets: land, roads and other infrastructure, buildings and improvements, vehicles, machinery and equipment.
Charges between departmentsCharacter 5 · Internal Charges
What city departments pay each other for shared services like the Mayor's Action Center, Corporation Counsel (legal) and Fleet Services. A negative number means that department provides the service and bills the others. County agencies don't use this category, and everyone pays for IT (ISA) under contracts and services.
Consolidated County / County GeneralThe main local tax funds, mostly property and income tax.
Public Safety Income TaxA local income tax dedicated to public safety, used heavily by the Sheriff and courts.
Cap Asset Lifecycle & DevA capital fund for long-lived assets. It carries much of the 2027 road match.

Reading the numbers

  • Since 2019, public safety spending is up 75% and infrastructure & community development is up 84%, against 54% for the budget overall. Criminal justice is up 41%.
  • 2023, 2024 and 2025 all ended with spending above revenue, covered by fund balance. The proposed 2027 budget is balanced on paper with a $43K surplus, and projects $402M in total fund balance at the end of 2027.
  • The 2027 increase leans on intergovernmental money (+$96M) and on property and income tax growth (+$88M combined). Some of the road match relies on a spring 2027 income-tax distribution that won't be certain until next year.
  • IndyGo, Health & Hospital Corporation (Eskenazi), the library and the airport budget separately and are not in these totals.
